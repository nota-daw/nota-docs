The Modular view draws a track's devices as a graph — **MIDI effects → instrument → effects →
track output** — and lets you patch **CV modulation** between them with cables. Switch to it
with **Modular** in the transport bar.

<Shot id="views/modular" alt="The Modular view showing the Keys track: Nota Pentad, EQ-8 and Chamber as nodes" />

## Track and Global

- **Track** shows the selected track's chain. Pick another track in the list on the left.
- **Global** shows every track as an island, with the connections between tracks.

Move around the canvas with <Kbd>Space</Kbd> + drag, zoom with <Kbd>⌘</Kbd> + scroll, and press
<Kbd>F</Kbd> (or **Fit**) to fit everything on screen. Node positions are saved with the project.

## Nodes

Each device is a node. On the canvas you can expand it, **bypass** it (<Kbd>B</Kbd>),
duplicate it with all its settings (<Kbd>⌘ D</Kbd>), delete it (the bin, or <Kbd>Delete</Kbd>)
and reorder it — the same chain you see in the detail panel. A node shows only the knobs that
have a CV patch attached; the chevron (or a double-click) shows them all. **Show fewer** under an
opened node, the chevron again or <Kbd>Esc</Kbd> closes it. Knobs on a node are the real
controls — turning them changes the sound.

The track's own nodes — its input (or instrument) and its output — carry a lock: they are where
the chain starts and ends, so they can't be deleted. To change an instrument, drop another one on
the track.

Cables are coloured by what they carry: audio, MIDI and CV each have their own colour.

## Modulators

**+ Add** puts a modulator on the canvas. Its **CV out** port is what you patch:

| Modulator | Puts out |
|---|---|
| **LFO** | A repeating wave — sine, triangle, saw, square or random — free or synced to the tempo, with a phase |
| **Envelope Follower** | The loudness of the track's audio — a peak controller |
| **MIDI → CV** | Note pitch, velocity or gate from the notes the track plays |
| **ADSR** | An attack–decay–sustain–release envelope, opened by each note |
| **Macro** | A knob you turn by hand (or map to a controller) |
| **Math** | Two CV inputs combined — **Add**, **Subtract**, **Multiply**, **Min**, **Max** — or one **Invert**ed or **Scale**d |

Any parameter can also act as a CV source: right-click a knob ▸ **Expose as CV output**, or
**Modulate from a parameter**. Nota Shutter's step pattern can be sent out as CV too.

## Patching

Drag a modulator's **CV out** onto a knob of any device, instrument or MIDI effect. A cable
links them. Click the cable to set its **depth** and **mode** — **Add** (on top of the knob's
position), **Multiply** (scaled by it) or **Override** (replacing it); select it and delete it
to remove it. Several modulations on one knob each get their own input and cable.

To modulate something on **another track**, drag the CV output onto that track in the list on
the left and pick a parameter — or right-click a knob ▸ **Modulate from another track**.

## Move a knob with another track's loudness

The **Envelope Follower** is Nota's peak controller: it turns how loud a track is into CV that can
drive any knob, on that track or another. The classic use is ducking — the bass dips each time the
kick hits:

1. Select the kick track and open **Modular**. **+ Add ▸ Envelope Follower**.
2. Set its **Attack** short (a few ms) so it catches the hit, and its **Release** to how long the
   dip should last (100–300 ms is a good start). **Depth** scales the whole signal.
3. On the bass track, right-click the knob to move — the Gain of
   [Nota Utility](../../devices/audio-effects/utility/), say, or a filter's cutoff ▸
   **Modulate from another track** ▸ the kick's follower (it's listed as **Env 1 · Kick**, say). (Or drag the follower's
   **CV out** onto the bass track in the list on the left.)
4. Click the cable and drag **depth** below zero: the knob now drops when the kick plays. A
   positive depth pushes it up instead.

**Global** shows the cable running between the two tracks. The follower reads the track's level
after its fader, so a muted track, or one with its fader all the way down, gives it nothing to
follow.

## CV Scope

Add a **Scope** to watch any modulator's signal as a moving line — handy for checking an LFO's
rate or how an envelope follower reacts. Drag the modulator's **CV out** onto the Scope node to
patch it in. The Scope shows CV, not audio: to watch a device's sound, put
[Nota Lens](../../devices/audio-effects/lens/) after it in the chain.

<Aside>
CV ports appear only where modulation actually works, so anything you can patch, you can hear.
</Aside>
