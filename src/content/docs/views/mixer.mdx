Show the Mixer with **View ▸ Mixer** or <Kbd>⌘ ⇧ M</Kbd>. It docks as a panel at the bottom of
the main window, under the Arrangement, Session or Modular view; its pop-out button moves it
into its own window, for a second screen, and the window's dock button brings it back.

<Shot id="views/mixer" alt="The Mixer window: a strip per track, the return and the master" />

## A channel strip

Top to bottom, each track gets:

- its **name** and colour;
- **I/O** — where the track listens: the audio input (**In 1** …) and **Monitor** (**Auto** or
  **In**) for audio tracks, **MIDI In** / **MIDI To** for instrument tracks; group and return
  strips show what feeds them;
- **Sends** — one bar per return track, with the amount written inside it;
- **Pan** and the **volume** fader with its meter. Drag the gain readout or double-click it to
  type a value; double-click the fader to reset it;
- **Mute**, **Solo** and **arm** buttons, and the **Cue** key (headphones) while you use
  [Cue](#cue-your-own-mix).

Strips come in the Arrangement's order — a group's strip, then its tracks. Return tracks come
after the tracks, then the **Master** strip, and the [Cue strip](#cue-your-own-mix) when you use
Cue. Its fader is the
same master volume as the **Master** control in the transport bar and the Master row of the
Arrangement — move one and the others follow; it is saved with the project. **Show** at the
bottom switches the **Sends** and **I/O** sections on or off; a section that is shown has its
chip lit.

## Groups

Each group puts a bar with its name, in its colour, over its own strip and its tracks' strips,
with a faint tint of the colour behind them. A group inside a group gets a second bar under the
first, so you can read which family every strip belongs to.

<Shot id="views/mixer-groups" alt="Group bars over the strips: Beat holds the Rhythm group and Bass, Rhythm holds Drums" />

Click the arrow on a group's strip to fold the group: its tracks hide and only the group strip
stays, so the rest of the song fits on screen. Folding a group here folds it in the
Arrangement too, and the other way round; folded groups are saved with the project. A folded
group's bar says how many strips it hides (**Beat +3**), and dots on its strip tell you when a
hidden track is soloed (blue), armed (red) or has clipped (click the dot to clear it). Folding
only changes what you see — the sound stays the same.

<Shot id="views/mixer-group-folded" alt="The Beat group folded: its bar says +3 and its strip is outlined because a hidden track is selected" />

The track you select anywhere gets an outline in the Mixer, and the Mixer scrolls to it. If
that track is inside a folded group, the group's strip is outlined instead; the group stays
folded.

## Compact strips

**Compact** in the Mixer's footer narrows every strip to 72 px, so about twice as many tracks
fit on screen; click it again for the regular strips. Nota remembers the choice.

<Shot id="views/mixer-compact" alt="Compact strips: a wide meter with a triangle for the volume, send and pan bars, full-width M, S and arm buttons" />

A compact strip has everything a regular one has, in a narrower shape:

- the **I/O** on one line (hover for the full routing);
- the **A** and **B** sends as thin bars;
- **Pan** as a bar that fills out from the centre — drag it up or down, double-click to centre;
- a wide level **meter** that is also the volume fader. The triangle on its right edge marks the
  volume on the meter's own scale, so a triangle at 0 sits at the meter's 0 dB. Drag anywhere on
  the meter to change it (hold ⇧ for fine steps); a click without dragging clears the peak hold;
- the gain value — drag it or double-click to type;
- **Mute**, **Solo**, **Cue** and arm as buttons across the whole strip.

MIDI Learn and automation recording work as on a regular strip.

## Cue: your own mix

Cue is a second, private mix on its own outputs — for your headphones on stage or in the
studio, while the audience or the room hears Main. Choose its output pair in
[Settings ▸ Outputs](../../preferences/outputs/#output-pairs); that needs an interface with
more than two outputs.

<Shot id="views/mixer-cue" alt="Keys and Lead in Cue, Keys muted; the Cue strip after Master plays on Out 3/4 and lists Keys, Lead + click" />

Press a track's **Cue** key (the headphones) to put it in Cue; press it again to take it out.
Any number of tracks can be in Cue at once. The key is on every track's strip here and in the
[Session mixer](../session/#the-mixer-under-the-grid), in the
[track headers](../../basics/tracks/) and in the track inspector.

- A track is heard in Cue **after its devices and before its fader, pan, mute and solo**. So a
  track muted in Main still plays in Cue — mute a guide or a click track for the audience and
  keep it in your ears. Your fader moves don't change what you hear in Cue.
- Sends, returns, groups and the master's devices don't shape Cue — you hear the track's own
  instrument and effects. With input monitoring on, an audio track's input is heard in Cue
  too, which is how you hear your vocal while you record it.
- A group or a return can be in Cue as well: its own submix and devices, before its fader. A
  muted group that is in Cue is heard in Cue only. A group and one of its tracks both in Cue
  are both heard, so that track comes in twice.
- Cue never reaches Main, a recording or an export: what you put in Cue changes nothing for
  the audience.

### The Cue strip

The **Cue** strip sits after Master. Top to bottom:

- its **output pair** (**Out 3/4 · Phones**) and the tracks in it — by name, or **3 tracks**
  when there are more than two, with **+ click** when the
  [click goes to Cue](../../basics/transport/#metronome-and-count-in). On a compact strip the
  tracks are in the tooltip;
- the **Cue level** fader with its meter — your listening level, from silent to +3.5 dB (0 dB
  by default). Drag the value below it or double-click it to type. It isn't an undo step and
  has no automation lane.

The strip and the Cue keys show only while a Cue output is set for this device or a track is
in Cue, so a laptop's plain stereo setup stays as it was. With tracks in Cue but no Cue output
the strip says **No cue output**.

<Shot id="views/mixer-cue-compact" alt="The same Cue mix with compact strips: the headphones key under M and S, the Cue strip last" />

If the device doesn't have the Cue pair — another device is chosen, or the interface is
unplugged — the strip says **Out 3/4 — not on this device** in orange, and Cue is silent:
nothing moves into Main. Choose another pair in Settings, or plug the interface back in. The
fader stays usable meanwhile.

<Shot id="views/mixer-cue-missing" alt="The Cue strip warns: Out 3/4 — not on this device" />

When Main isn't on outputs 1/2, the Master strip's I/O line shows its pair (**→ Out 3/4 ·
…**). If Main's own pair is missing, Main plays on 1/2 and the line reads **→ Out 1/2
(fallback)**.

## Sends and returns

A return track is a shared effect bus. Add one (right-click the empty space below the tracks ▸
**Add return track**), put a Reverb or Delay on it, then turn up each track's send to it.
Every track goes to the same reverb, and one fader on the return sets how much of it you hear.

The same sends are in the Arrangement's track headers at the Large height — see
[Sends in the track headers](../arrangement/#sends-in-the-track-headers). A send move is not an
undo step, and a send has no automation lane.

<Aside>
The Session view has the same mixer under its grid — see [Session](../session/#the-mixer-under-the-grid).
Track volume, pan, mute and solo are also right in each [track header](../../basics/tracks/).
</Aside>
