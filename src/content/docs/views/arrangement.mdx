The Arrangement lays the song out left to right: one row per track, time running along the
ruler. It is where a song gets its final shape.

<Shot id="views/arrangement-timeline" alt="The Arrangement with the whole demo song in view: the Rhythm group, Keys, Lead and Texture" />

From top to bottom: the **Overview** strip, the **Sections** lane, the ruler with the
**Zoom** buttons, the tracks, and — pinned at the bottom — the return tracks and the master.

## Clips

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Double-click empty space on an instrument track | Create a MIDI clip there |
| Click a clip | Select it |
| Drag a clip | Move it — along the track, or to another track of the same kind |
| Drag clips into the empty space below the last track | Move them to a new track — one per source track, with the same instrument, devices and mix |
| <Kbd>←</Kbd> <Kbd>→</Kbd> | Nudge the selected clips by the grid step (<Kbd>⌥</Kbd>: by a 64th note) |
| <Kbd>⌥ ↑</Kbd> <Kbd>⌥ ↓</Kbd> | Move the selected clips to the next track up or down that takes them, at the same beats |
| Drag a clip's left or right edge | Trim it |
| Drag with <Kbd>⌥</Kbd> | Ignore the grid for this drag |
| Double-click a clip | Open it in the [clip editor](../../editing/piano-roll/) |
| <Kbd>⌘</Kbd>-click | Add or remove a clip from the selection |
| Drag across empty space | Select every clip the rectangle touches |
| <Kbd>⌘ A</Kbd> | Select all clips |

Clips snap to the [snap grid](../../basics/transport/#the-snap-grid) while you drag them —
in **Auto** it follows the zoom, so they land on the lines you see — and the edges of
neighbouring clips pull them in. The magnet button (<Kbd>⌘ 4</Kbd>) switches snapping off. Dragging several selected clips moves them together, as one undo step.

While you drag clips below the last track, a **New track** ring shows where they will land.
On release each track the clips came from gets a new track like it, placed above the returns,
and the clips move there; one undo takes back both. The arrow keys move the whole selection
as a block too: a nudge left stops at the song start, and <Kbd>⌥ ↑</Kbd> / <Kbd>⌥ ↓</Kbd> skip
tracks that can't hold the clips (audio clips go on audio tracks, MIDI clips on instrument
tracks). Each key press is one undo step.

Right-click a clip for **Split here**, **Duplicate**, **Delete**, **Deactivate clip**,
**Reverse** (audio), **Consolidate**, **Copy** / **Cut** / **Paste**, **Rename…**, **Loop
selection**, **Copy to Session** / **Copy to scene**, and — on audio — **Separate stems…** and
**Convert** ([Convert audio to MIDI](../../editing/midi-tools/)). Most of these work on the
whole selection at once.

<Aside type="tip">
<Kbd>0</Kbd> deactivates the selected clips: they stay in place, greyed out, and play
nothing. <Kbd>0</Kbd> again switches them back on. A quick way to try a part without it.
</Aside>

## Time ranges

<Kbd>⇧</Kbd>-drag anywhere in the lanes — even starting on a clip — to select a stretch of
time across tracks instead of whole clips. A range is what [Duplicate time, Insert silence,
Split and Consolidate](../../editing/time-ranges/) work on, and <Kbd>⌘ L</Kbd> loops it.

## Moving around

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| <Kbd>⌘</Kbd> + scroll | Zoom in and out around the mouse pointer |
| <Kbd>⇧</Kbd> + scroll, or a sideways swipe | Scroll along the song |
| Scroll | Scroll through the tracks |
| **Zoom −** / **+** | Zoom out / in |
| Click in the ruler | Move the playhead |
| Drag along the ruler | Draw a loop range |
| Double-click the loop range | Turn looping off or on; the range stays |

### The overview strip

The **Overview** above the ruler shows the whole song in miniature, with the part on screen
as a window over it. Drag the window sideways to scroll, drag it up or down — or drag its
edges — to zoom, and double-click the strip to fit the whole song on screen. The label next to
it reads which bars you're looking at ("bar 1–8 / 36"). **View ▸ Toggle overview** hides it.

## Sections

The **Sections** lane names the parts of the song — Intro, Verse, Drop — so the structure is
readable at a glance.

- Drag along the empty lane to mark a section over those bars. It gets an automatic name.
- Click a section to jump the playhead to its start; double-click it to rename it.
- Drag a section to move it, drag its edge to resize it — always in whole bars.
- Right-click it for **Loop section**, **Select section** (as a time range across every track),
  **Duplicate**, **Rename…**, **Reset to default name** and **Delete**.

Each of these edits is one undo step. Sections are saved with the project. **View ▸ Toggle
sections** hides the lane.

### Section names

A new section is named **Section N**, where N is its place in the song counted from the left.
This is an *automatic* name: it renumbers when you add, move, duplicate or delete sections. A
section you named yourself is counted too — if the second section is "Chorus", the third is
still **Section 3** — and its name never changes on its own.

Double-click a section (or right-click ▸ **Rename…**) to open the rename box:

- For an automatic section the box opens empty, with the automatic name shown as a hint. A
  caption under the box reads **Automatic name** or **Custom name**, following what you type.
- <Kbd>Return</Kbd> keeps the name. It is one undo step, or none if nothing changed. Leading and
  trailing spaces are trimmed, and an empty name goes back to automatic.
- <Kbd>Esc</Kbd>, or a click anywhere else, cancels and changes nothing.
- **Reset to default** in the box gives the section its automatic name back. It is available
  only for a custom name.

**Reset to default name** in the section's right-click menu does the same, and is greyed out
while the name is already automatic. From the keyboard, open the [command
palette](../../basics/command-palette/) and run **Reset section name**: it acts on the section
under the playhead and says in the status bar what it did.

Undo and redo bring back both the name and whether it was automatic. Sections you named in
earlier versions of Nota — including ones called "Section 2" — load as custom names; reset one
to make it automatic.

## Track sizes

Make tracks short to see a big song at once, or tall to work on one part.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Drag a track's bottom edge (in its header or its lane) | Make it taller or shorter, from 30 to 256 px; it snaps lightly to the three sizes below |
| The same with several tracks selected | Resize them all together |
| <Kbd>⌥</Kbd> + drag a bottom edge | Resize every track at once |
| Double-click a bottom edge | Go back to the standard height |
| Right-click a track ▸ **Track height** | Pick **Compact**, **Standard** or **Large** for the selection, or for all tracks |
| Drag the seam between the track headers and the lanes | Widen or narrow the headers (160–480 px); double-click it to reset |

The header shows what fits. A **compact** track keeps its name and mute, solo and record-arm
on one line; volume, pan and the input stay in the [Mixer](../mixer/) and the track's menu. A
**standard** track has the usual three rows. A **large** track gives the input (or MIDI
source), the volume fader and the pan a row each. Clips, waveforms and automation grow with
the row.

Track heights and the header width are saved with the project. They don't go into the undo
history.

## Clip names and groups

A clip shows its name only where a run of clips begins, so a repeated pattern reads as one
block; a clip you named by hand always shows its name. **View ▸ Clip names** switches between
every clip, the first of each run, and none.

Group tracks collapse to a slim titled bar with mute, solo and level, and open to their full
controls when selected — see [Tracks](../../basics/tracks/#groups).

## Arrangement and Session together

Clips move between the Arrangement and [Session](../session/) with the clipboard or
<Kbd>⌘ ⇧ C</Kbd> — see [Copy, paste and drag](../../editing/clipboard/). When a track plays
Session clips, its Arrangement clips wait; **Back to Arrangement** in the Session toolbar hands
the tracks back.
