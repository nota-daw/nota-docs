Nota recognises many controllers when you plug them in. Their transport buttons, faders and knobs
work right away, so you don't need to map anything with [MIDI Learn](../midi-learn/). Keys and
pads keep playing as before.

## Controllers Nota knows

Nota recognises a controller by a **profile**: its port name and, if it answers, its MIDI identity.
Profiles ship for:

- **Arturia KeyLab Essential 61 mk3**: in its **DAW** program the knobs, faders, transport,
  track arrows, main encoder and the Undo, Redo, Metro, Save, Quantize and Tap buttons all work;
  keys and pads keep playing. If you set the transport mode to MCU in MIDI Control Center, its
  **MCU/HUI** port works as a Mackie Control surface instead.
- **Novation Launchkey MK3** (25, 37, 49, 61, 88): its **DAW** port. Nota switches the keyboard
  into DAW mode when it connects and back when it lets go.
- **Behringer X-Touch Mini**: Standard mode, layer A.
- **Mackie Control**: any surface whose port is called MCU, Mackie Control, X-Touch, iCON or
  Platform M.

When a controller is recognised, the status bar says so: "‹port› connected — using the ‹profile›
profile".

## What it does

- **Transport**: Play, Stop, Record, Loop, Rewind and Forward (a bar back or forward). The
  transport lights follow Nota.
- **Faders** move the volume of a **bank of eight tracks**. The tracks are taken in Mixer order:
  tracks with their groups, then the returns. The bank buttons shift the bank by eight. The tracks
  under the faders have an orange mark on their headers and an orange outline in the Mixer.
- **Track buttons** select the previous or next track, in every view, as a click on its header
  does: the knobs, the Devices panel and the Mixer follow. Members of a folded group and return
  tracks are skipped. The bank moves along so the selected track stays under the faders.
- **Master fader** moves the master volume.
- **Knobs** follow the selected device (see below).

A fader or knob move is one undo step and writes automation like the on-screen control. If you
map a control with MIDI Learn, your mapping wins over the profile.

## Knobs follow the selected device

The eight knobs control the **selected device**. With nothing selected they control the selected
track's instrument, or its first device if the track has no instrument. This also works when the
Devices panel is closed: selecting a track in the Arrangement or the Mixer is enough. Devices inside
racks work too.

Each device has **pages** of up to eight parameters, the ones you play first (filter, tone,
envelope), and the page buttons step through them. Every built-in instrument, effect and MIDI
effect has pages. A rack's page is its eight macros. A plug-in gets its parameters in order, eight
per page.

On the device's card, the eight controls of the current page show their knob number and an orange
underline. Knob 1 also shows the page, for example **2 / 3 · Filter**. The card itself has an
orange line along its top and a **Controller** tag.

### See what the knobs are on

The controller chip next to **MIDI** in the transport bar always says what the knobs control:
the track, the device and the page, for example **KeyLab · Bass · Nota Volt · 2 / 4**. It changes
the moment you select another track or device or turn a page, in every view. In the Session, where
the lower panel shows the selected clip, this is how you see which device you are playing. Click
the chip and choose **Show device** to open it on the Devices panel.

### Step through a track's devices

**Next device** and **Previous device** move the knobs along the track's chain: the MIDI effects,
then the instrument, then the audio effects. A rack counts as one device. The device is selected
and scrolled into view, and its first page comes up. At either end the step does nothing. Give the
two commands controller buttons in **Set up…** (the **Devices** step) or from a button's
right-click menu, or keys in **Settings ▸ Shortcuts**. They are in the command palette too.

### Knob modes

The knobs have four modes:

- **Device**: the selected device's page (the default).
- **Volume** and **Pan**: the volume or pan of the eight tracks in the bank.
- **Send A, B, …**: one send level of the bank's tracks. Return tracks are skipped.

Switch modes with the controller's mode buttons, if its profile has them. You can also click the
controller chip next to **MIDI** in the transport bar and pick a mode from its menu. In
**Volume**, **Pan** and **Send** the chip shows the mode and the bank, for example
**KeyLab · Volume · tracks 9–16**. Each controller
keeps its own bank, page and mode. Opening a project goes back to the first bank, the first page
and Device mode.

## No jumps: pickup

A knob or fader may not be where the value is: you switched devices, pages or banks, or you moved
the value with the mouse, automation or undo. Nota then waits until the hardware reaches the value
(or passes it) and only then takes over, so nothing jumps. Until the control picks up, a hollow
dot shows where the hardware is: on a knob it sits on the knob's arc (the knob's pointer is the
value); on a fader or slider it sits on a thin rail beside it, with an orange tick at the value.

Motor faders and endless encoders don't need this. To switch pickup off for a controller, use
Settings (below); the value then jumps to the control, as with MIDI Learn.

## Mackie Control and HUI surfaces

A Mackie Control surface (or a keyboard's MCU port) gets the full layout:

- **Channel strips** for the bank's eight tracks: fader = volume, V-pot = what the knobs do (the
  knob mode above; push it in Pan mode to centre the pan), **Rec**, **Solo**, **Mute** and
  **Select**, the track name on the scribble strip, and the track's meter.
- **Master fader**, **transport**, **bank** and **channel** buttons, and the **jog wheel** (moves
  the playhead a sixteenth per step while stopped).
- The **time display** shows bars, beats, sixteenths and ticks. On the bottom scribble line, the
  name of each V-pot's parameter is shown.

Everything you change in Nota (a mute, a fader drawn by automation, a renamed track) shows up on
the surface. Nota sends only what changed, plus everything when the surface connects, the bank
moves or a project opens. **Touching a motor fader** is a movement of its own: automation is
written and the undo step lasts exactly as long as you hold it, and Nota doesn't drive that fader
while your hand is on it.

**HUI** surfaces work the same way, with four-letter strip names and no time display. Nota checks
every second that a HUI surface is still there and refreshes it when it comes back.

When Nota quits, it blanks the scribble strips, drops the faders and switches the lights off.

## Settings ▸ MIDI ▸ Controllers

<Shot id="control/controllers-settings" alt="Settings, MIDI page: the Controllers list with a KeyLab and an X-Touch Mini recognised, two plain ports with Set up…, the profile menus and the Pickup switches" width={700} />

Each connected port is listed with:

- a menu to pick its profile: **Automatic** (shows the profile Nota found), any profile, **Mackie
  Control (any surface)** or **HUI (any surface)** to drive any port as a surface, or **None —
  plain MIDI**;
- **Pickup**, on by default;
- a switch to turn the controller off. It is then plain MIDI again for playing and MIDI Learn.

A port that has no matching MIDI output can't get feedback. Settings says so below the list.

**Reload profiles** re-reads the profile files, and **Open profiles folder** opens the folder for
your own. Problems in a profile file (a bad control, a newer format) are listed below the buttons.

## Set up a controller Nota doesn't know

<Shot id="control/controllers-setup" alt="The Set up window on step 2: the group strip at the top with Knobs current, a knob picture, the prompt to turn knob 2, the last message received, and knob 1 learned as CC 16" width={600} />

<Steps>

1. In **Settings ▸ MIDI ▸ Controllers**, click **Set up…** next to the controller. After that the
   button reads **Set up again…**, to go through the controls once more. On a controller Nota
   already knows, **Set up…** makes your own profile, which replaces the built-in one.

2. Do what each step asks. A picture shows the kind of control: a knob, a fader, a button with
   its icon, the jog wheel. Turn knob 1 right, then left, and the next step comes by itself; press
   Play, and the next step comes. **Hearing** shows what the controller sends as it arrives. Nota
   works out what the control sends: note, CC, 14-bit CC, NRPN or pitch bend, and for an endless
   encoder its encoding. If it can't tell the encoding, it asks you to pick it and click **Next**.

3. **Skip** a control your controller doesn't have, or **No more knobs** (faders, buttons…) to skip
   the rest of a group. **Back** redoes the step before, **Try again** this one. A control you
   already set up is ignored, and the window says which step it belongs to.

   After the knobs, faders and transport come the track, bank, page and device controls, the **Session**
   group and extras: Undo / Redo, Tempo − / +, Metronome, Save, Tap tempo and the jog wheel.

   Every pair of opposite actions — previous / next track, bank, page and device, rewind / forward, the
   Session's up / down and left / right, undo / redo, tempo − / + — is one step, and it can be an
   encoder or two buttons: turn an encoder and both halves are set (right is next, down, forward,
   redo, faster); press a button and it is the first half, then the window asks for the other one.
   The Session group also has launch / stop clip, stop track, launch scene and stop all.

   The groups along the top show where you are; click one to go there. Each learned control
   appears as a chip under the step; click it to learn it again.

   <Shot id="control/controllers-setup-pair" alt="A pair step in the Session group: an encoder and two buttons pictured, with the prompt to turn an encoder for up and down the clips or press the Up button" width={600} />

4. Click **Finish**. Nota saves the profile in your profiles folder and uses it at once.

</Steps>

While the window is open, Nota listens only to that controller and nothing plays.

## Play the Session from the controller

While the **Session** view shows, the jog wheel (on the KeyLab, the big encoder) walks the selected
track's clips, and the track buttons move the selection to the same scene of the next or
previous track. A launch button — the
KeyLab's encoder push, or one you set up — launches the selected clip; pressed again it stops it
(or withdraws a launch that is still waiting). Set up… also learns buttons for moving up, down,
left and right, stopping the track, launching the scene and stopping all clips. In the Arrangement
these buttons do nothing and the jog wheel moves the playhead.

## Change what a control drives

Right-click a control on a device card, in the Mixer or a transport button, and open
**Controller** (or, if the control has no menu of its own, the menu opens with these items):

- The first line names the hardware control that drives it now, for example **Knob 4 · Arturia
  KeyLab Essential 61 mk3 (DAW)**, or says no controller control does.
- **Unbind** stops that hardware control driving this parameter only. On a device the knob's slot
  on the page becomes empty; in the Mixer the bank's fader skips this track; a transport button
  lets go of the hardware button, so it reaches MIDI Learn like any other control.
- **Bind to ▸** lists the controller's controls by name. For a device parameter pick a knob: the
  parameter takes that knob's slot on the current page, and the one that was there leaves it. For
  anything else pick a fader, knob or button: it drives this control from now on, whatever the
  bank. Endless encoders and Mackie Control strips aren't offered here.
- **Back to default** undoes your change (for a device: **Back to this device's default pages**).

Device changes are saved as the device's pages (below). A change to a track's control is saved with
the project; a change to a transport or master control applies in every project, and each project
keeps a copy.

## Edit a device's pages

Right-click a device's header and choose **Edit controller pages…**. The window lists the device's
pages on the left; add, remove, rename and reorder them, and pick each of the eight knobs from the
device's parameters. A parameter that no longer exists (renamed by an update) is struck through so
you can replace it. **Save** applies the pages to every device of that kind. **Reset pages**
brings back Nota's own.

Edited pages are saved for you and also in each project that uses that device, so a project opens
with the same pages on another computer. While a project is open, its pages win.

## Your own profiles

A profile is a JSON file in the `controllers` folder of Nota's data directory. The setup wizard
writes one; you can also write or edit one by hand, for example to add the messages that switch a
keyboard into its DAW mode, or LED feedback.

```jsonc
{
  "format": 1,
  "id": "me.my-keys",                      // a-z, 0-9, dots and dashes
  "name": "My Keys",
  "match": { "portNames": ["^My Keys DAW"] },   // regex on the port name, and/or "identity"
  "init":    ["9F 0C 7F"],                 // sent on connect (hex)
  "release": ["9F 0C 00"],                 // sent when Nota lets go
  "pickup": true,
  "controls": [
    { "role": "knob",  "index": 1, "message": "cc", "channel": 16, "number": 21 },
    { "role": "fader", "index": 1, "message": "cc", "channel": 16, "number": 53 },
    { "role": "play",  "message": "cc", "channel": 16, "number": 115,
      "feedback": { "on": "BF 73 7F", "off": "BF 73 00" } }
  ]
}
```

- **role**: `knob`, `fader` (with `index` 1–8), `master-fader`, `play`, `stop`, `record`, `loop`,
  `rewind`, `forward`, `page-next` / `page-prev`, `bank-next` / `bank-prev`, `track-next` /
  `track-prev`, `mode-device`, `mode-volume`, `mode-pan`, `mode-send`, `jog`, `command`. `pad` and `button`
  are noted but stay free for playing and MIDI Learn.
- **command** (for `role: "command"`): the Nota command the button runs, by its id — for example
  `edit.undo`, `edit.redo`, `file.save`, `transport.metronome`, `transport.tapTempo`,
  `roll.quantize`, `session.toggleSlot`. A command that can't run at the moment (nothing to undo)
  does nothing. **roleLeft** makes an encoder do both halves of a button pair (`track-next` with
  `roleLeft: "track-prev"`; also banks, pages and `forward` / `rewind`). Add **commandLeft** to
  make an encoder or knob two-way for commands: `command` runs once per
  step to the right, `commandLeft` once per step to the left (`session.selectDown` /
  `session.selectUp`).
- **message**: `cc`, `cc14`, `nrpn`, `note`, `pitchbend`; **channel** 1–16.
- **encoder** for endless knobs: `relative-twos`, `relative-signbit`, `relative-offset` (default
  `absolute`).
- **protocol** `"mcu"` or `"hui"` makes the port a whole Mackie Control / HUI surface (no
  `controls` needed).

A profile of yours with the same `id` as one of Nota's replaces it. After editing, click **Reload
profiles**.
