MIDI Learn connects a physical control to a control on screen: turn a knob on your controller and a
filter moves in Nota.

## Mapping a control

<Steps>

1. <At id="ui:transport.midi-learn" /> Click **MIDI** in the transport bar. Everything you can map lights up.

2. Click the control in Nota you want to move — a knob, fader, switch, macro…

3. Move a knob or fader on your controller (or press a pad, a gamepad button, or move a phone
   control). The mapping is made; mapped controls stay outlined in green.

4. Map more, then click **MIDI** again to finish.

</Steps>

## What you can map

Every parameter of the built-in instruments, effects and MIDI effects (switches, envelopes and
filters included), plug-in parameters, track volume, pan, mute, solo and [Cue](../../views/mixer/#cue-your-own-mix), the
master and the Cue level, the transport,
rack macros, the mute and solo of rack chains and Drum Rack pads, and the
[Session](../../views/session/)'s clip and scene launch buttons.

Sources can be a **CC**, a **MIDI note**, a **gamepad** button, stick or trigger (macOS), or a control on
a phone running [Nota Remote](../nota-remote/) — all in one table.

- A **button or note** is momentary: it flips a switch once when pressed, and holds a continuous
  control at the top of its range until released.
- A **knob, fader, stick or trigger** drives a continuous control over its whole travel, and flips a
  switch as it crosses halfway.

## Session clips and scenes

With MIDI Learn on, the [Session](../../views/session/) grid's launch buttons light up: each clip's
**▶**, an empty slot's stop / record button, and each scene's **▶**. Click one, then hit a pad, a key
or a button. The pad then does what a click on that button does, whether the Session is showing or
not:

- a **clip** launches at its quantization, with the pad's velocity (scaled by the clip's velocity
  amount); letting go of the pad stops a **Gate** or **Repeat** clip;
- an **empty slot** records on an armed track, otherwise it stops the track (when the slot has its
  stop button);
- a slot that is **recording** stops its take;
- a **scene** launches its whole row.

A mapping belongs to a place in the grid — a track and a row — like a pad on a pad grid: move or
insert scenes and it launches whatever sits in that row now. The MIDI map lists it as
**Launch · Bass · Scene 2** (or **Launch · Scene 2** for a scene).

## The MIDI map

<Shot id="control/midi-map-tab" alt="The MIDI map tab of the browser" width={300} />

The browser's **MIDI map** tab lists every mapping: the control, its source (`CC 74 · ch1`, `Note 36 ·
ch10`, `Pad · Left stick ↑↓`, `Phone · XY X`), an editable output **range**, **Invert**, and delete.
Mappings are saved with the project.
A track's Cue key is listed as **Cue**, like a track's **Mute**, and the Cue strip's fader as
**Cue level**.
