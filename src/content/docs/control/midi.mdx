Plug in a MIDI keyboard or controller and play: Nota listens to every connected MIDI input by
default. Notes go to the selected instrument track; arm the track and press Record to
[record](../../editing/recording/) them.

## Choosing inputs

**Settings ▸ MIDI** lists the connected MIDI inputs. Switch one off to stop Nota listening to it; new
devices are on by default. Plug a controller in while Settings is open and reopen it to see it.

<Shot id="preferences/midi" alt="Settings ▸ MIDI with the list of MIDI inputs" />

The **MIDI** button in the transport bar has a dot that flashes when a controller sends something —
handy for checking a controller that's connected but silent.

## No MIDI keyboard? Use the computer keyboard

| Keys | Play |
|---|---|
| <Kbd>A</Kbd> <Kbd>S</Kbd> <Kbd>D</Kbd> <Kbd>F</Kbd> <Kbd>G</Kbd> <Kbd>H</Kbd> <Kbd>J</Kbd> <Kbd>K</Kbd> | White keys, from C4 |
| <Kbd>W</Kbd> <Kbd>E</Kbd> · <Kbd>T</Kbd> <Kbd>Y</Kbd> <Kbd>U</Kbd> | Black keys |
| <Kbd>Z</Kbd> / <Kbd>X</Kbd> | Octave down / up |
| <Kbd>C</Kbd> / <Kbd>V</Kbd> | Velocity down / up |

It works even while a plug-in's window has focus. The pressed key lights up on the piano roll.

## Knobs and faders

To make a controller's knobs, faders and pads move Nota's controls, use [MIDI Learn](../midi-learn/).
A gamepad and your phone can play and control Nota too — see [Gamepads](../gamepads/) and
[Nota Remote](../nota-remote/).
