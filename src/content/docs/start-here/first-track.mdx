This walk-through takes you from an empty project to a short loop exported as a WAV file.
It uses only what comes with Nota — no plug-ins, no samples.

## 1. Start a project

On the start window click **New project** (or press <Kbd>⌘ N</Kbd> in the main window). You
get an empty Arrangement at 120 BPM in 4/4.

## 2. Add an instrument

<Steps>

1. In the browser, open the **Instruments** tab (the keyboard icon at the top of the rail).

2. Double-click **Nota Volt**. Nota adds an instrument track with Volt on it and an empty
   one-bar MIDI clip at the start of the song.

</Steps>

<Shot id="start-here/browser-instruments" alt="The Instruments tab of the browser" width={300} />

Want to hear what an instrument sounds like first? Select it and press the play button in the
preview strip at the bottom of the browser.

## 3. Write a few notes

<Steps>

1. Double-click the clip on the timeline. It opens in the **Clip** tab of the panel at the
   bottom.

2. Double-click an empty spot of the grid to add a note there; double-click a note to delete
   it. Drag a note to move it, drag its edge to make it longer or shorter.

3. The lane under the notes is velocity — drag a stem up or down to play that note louder
   or softer.

</Steps>

<Shot id="start-here/clip-editor" alt="A MIDI clip open in the clip editor, with notes and their velocity stems" />

The **Grid** on the left sets what notes snap to; **Quantize** pulls the selected notes onto
it. With notes selected, the arrow keys move them by the grid (<Kbd>←</Kbd> <Kbd>→</Kbd>) or a
semitone (<Kbd>↑</Kbd> <Kbd>↓</Kbd>), and <Kbd>⇧ ↑</Kbd> / <Kbd>⇧ ↓</Kbd> move them an octave.

<Aside type="tip">
No MIDI keyboard? Play notes on your computer keyboard: <Kbd>A</Kbd> to <Kbd>K</Kbd> are the
white keys from C4, <Kbd>W</Kbd> <Kbd>E</Kbd> <Kbd>T</Kbd> <Kbd>Y</Kbd> <Kbd>U</Kbd> the black
ones, <Kbd>Z</Kbd> / <Kbd>X</Kbd> shift the octave.
</Aside>

## 4. Listen in a loop

<Steps>

1. Click the clip on the timeline to select it and press <Kbd>⌘ L</Kbd> — the loop is set to
   the clip and switched on.

2. Press <Kbd>Space</Kbd> to play, and again to stop. Keep editing notes while it plays —
   you hear each change straight away.

</Steps>

To make the part longer, select the clip and press <Kbd>⌘ D</Kbd> to put a copy right after
it, or drag the clip's right edge.

## 5. Add an effect

<Steps>

1. Keep the Volt track selected and open the **Audio effects** tab of the browser.

2. Double-click **Nota Delay**. It appears in the track's device chain, after Volt.

3. Turn its knobs while the loop plays. Drag a knob up or down; hold <Kbd>⇧</Kbd> for fine
   steps; double-click it to go back to the default.

</Steps>

<Shot id="start-here/browser-effects" alt="The Audio effects tab of the browser" width={300} />

Every device has factory presets — click the preset name in the device's header to browse
them.

## 6. Save

Press <Kbd>⌘ S</Kbd>, give the project a name and choose where to put it. Projects saved in
**Documents ▸ Nota Projects** also show up in the browser's **Projects** tab. A project is a
single `.nota` folder that holds everything it uses, samples included, so you can move or
back it up as one item. Each save also keeps a [version](../../basics/version-history/) you
can go back to.

## 7. Export a WAV

<Steps>

1. Choose **File ▸ Export audio…** (<Kbd>⌘ ⇧ E</Kbd>).

2. Under **Range**, keep **Loop** to export just the loop, or pick **Full song**.

3. Leave **WAV**, **48 000** and **24-bit**, or change them, and click **Export WAV**.

</Steps>

<Shot id="export/export-window" alt="The Export Audio window" width={280} />

**Add 1-bar release tail** keeps the delay ringing out after the last note. **Export stems**
writes one file per track instead of the mix. More in [Export audio](../../export/export-audio/).
