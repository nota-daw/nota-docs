The transport bar runs along the top of the main window. It holds what plays the song and
the settings that apply to the whole project.

<Shot id="basics/transport-bar" alt="The transport bar" />

## Play, stop, record

| Control | Key | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Play** | <Kbd>Space</Kbd> | Start playback; <Kbd>Space</Kbd> again stops. |
| **Pause** | <Kbd>⇧ Space</Kbd> | Stop and leave the playhead where it is; the next Play carries on from there. |
| **Stop** | <Kbd>Return</Kbd> | Stop, Session clips included. Press it again to jump back to the start of the song. |
| **Record** | <Kbd>⌘ R</Kbd> | Record onto the armed tracks — see [Recording](../../editing/recording/). |

When you stop, the playhead returns to where playback started, so you can listen to the same
passage again and again. To stop where you are instead, pause with <Kbd>⇧ Space</Kbd> (also
**Transport ▸ Play / pause**). Click in the ruler to move the playhead somewhere else.

The **position** readout shows bars.beats.sixteenths. Click it to switch to minutes:seconds
and back. The rulers follow it: the Arrangement's and both clip editors' read in time while the
readout does.

## Tempo, time signature and key

- **BPM** — drag up or down to change the tempo in whole steps, or double-click it and type a
  value with up to two decimals. The range is 20–300 BPM.
- **Tap** — tap the beat on it to set the tempo by ear. The tempo follows from the second tap
  and settles as you go on (it averages the last eight); the four dots count the beats. Change
  pace and it picks up the new tempo right away; pause for three seconds to start a new series.
  It is also in the command palette as **Tap tempo**, and on a narrow window it is the first of
  these cells to step aside.
- **Time signature** — drag each number; the lower one moves in powers of two
  (2, 4, 8, 16).
- **Key** — the project key, saved with the project. Click it to pick a key, or **No key**.
  With **Transpose to project key** on in the Files tab's menu, loops you drop on the
  Arrangement are transposed to it; loops are always warped to the project tempo. The phone
  keyboard of [Nota Remote](../../control/nota-remote/) uses it too.

## Loop

The **Loop** button (two arrows chasing each other) turns looping on or off for the range set
on the ruler; <Kbd>⌘ L</Kbd> does the same — and
if clips or a time range are selected, it first sets the loop to them. Drag along the ruler to
draw a loop range by hand. Double-click the loop range on the ruler to turn looping off without
losing the range: it stays on the ruler in grey, and another double-click turns it back on.

To change the range, drag one of its ends on the ruler: the cursor turns into a sideways arrow
and the edge brightens where you can grab it, and the other end stays put. Drag the range
itself to move it without changing its length. Both snap to the grid; hold <Kbd>⌥</Kbd> to
place them freely. While you drag, the ruler shows the start, the end and the length.

**Right-click** the ruler to turn looping on or off, loop the selection, or **Clear loop
region**. Clearing forgets the range (turning the loop off keeps it); clips, notes and
automation are not touched.

Notes you hold across the loop end keep sounding, and release tails ring on into the next pass.

The loop-record button next to it (<Kbd>⌘ ⇧ R</Kbd>) switches between **Overdub** (two stacked
layers) and **Replace** (one layer with a turn arrow, tinted). It sets how MIDI recorded in the
loop combines from pass to pass — see [Recording over a loop](../../editing/recording/#recording-over-a-loop).

## Metronome and count-in

The metronome button (<Kbd>⌘ M</Kbd>) clicks on every beat while you play or record.

**Right-click** it to choose a **count-in**: off, 1, 2 or 4 bars of clicks before Play and
Record from stop, and before a Session slot records from stop. The count-in sounds even with the metronome off, the position readout counts
the bars before the start (−2.1, −1.4 …), and the clicks are never recorded. Stop cancels a
count-in. Nota remembers the setting.

## Grid, snap and follow

- **Grid** — the Arrangement's snap grid: it shows the step clips snap to right now (**Auto
  grid** under it while it follows the zoom). Click it for the grid menu — see
  [The snap grid](#the-snap-grid).
- **Snap** (the magnet) — clips snap to the grid while you drag them (<Kbd>⌘ 4</Kbd>). Hold
  <Kbd>⌥</Kbd> while dragging to place a clip freely just this once.
- **Follow** — the Arrangement scrolls to keep the playhead in view. Also in **View ▸ Follow
  playhead**. Scroll away while it plays and Follow pauses so you can look around: the button
  turns into an outline, and <Kbd>P</Kbd>, a click on the button or the next Play picks it up
  again. It never scrolls under a clip you are dragging.
- **Show playhead** — <Kbd>P</Kbd> (or **View ▸ Show playhead**) scrolls the Arrangement to the
  playhead without moving it. When the playhead is off screen, an arrow at that end of the
  ruler points to it; click the arrow to do the same.
- **Automation** — shows the automation lanes of the tracks (<Kbd>⌘ ⇧ A</Kbd>). The button
  next to it hands controls you've moved by hand back to their automation — see
  [Automation](../../editing/automation/).

### The snap grid

The Arrangement and the clip editors each have a snap grid — the Grid cell here, a chip in
the clip editor's header there (the piano roll and the audio clip editor share one setting).
The two settings are separate and Nota remembers them.

- **Auto** (the default) — the step follows the zoom, so you snap to the lines you see:
  zoom in for eighths and sixteenths, zoom out and clips move by whole bars. **Narrow**,
  **Medium** and **Wide** set how dense the lines get.
- **A fixed step** — 1/32, 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1 bar, 2 bars or 4 bars, whatever the zoom.
  When it is finer than the lines on screen, its label dims: zoom in to see it.
- **Triplets** — three steps where there were two (1/8T, 1/16T …), in Auto and fixed alike.
- **Snap to grid** — the same switch as the magnet.

| Keys | Do |
|---|---|
| <Kbd>⌘ 1</Kbd> · <Kbd>⌘ 2</Kbd> | Narrow · widen the grid (in Auto, the density) |
| <Kbd>⌘ 3</Kbd> | Triplets on / off |
| <Kbd>⌘ 4</Kbd> | Snap on / off |
| <Kbd>⌘ 5</Kbd> | Switch between Auto and the last fixed step |

The keys act on the editor you used last — the Arrangement, or the clip editor (piano roll
or audio) once you've clicked into it — and the status bar says which. While the audio clip
editor shows **ADSR** or an unwarped clip, nothing there follows a grid, so the keys act on the
Arrangement. They are also in **Edit ▸
Grid**.

## The right side

- **MIDI** — [MIDI Learn](../../control/midi-learn/): click it, click a control in Nota, then
  move a knob on your controller.
- **Remote** — shows the QR code that connects a phone ([Nota Remote](../../control/nota-remote/)).
- **DSP** — how much of each audio buffer the engine needs. Near 100% you'll hear dropouts; raise
  the buffer size or [freeze](../tracks/) a heavy track. Hover it to see the CPU used by the
  whole Nota process, in the same scale as your system monitor.
- **Master** — master volume, with the master meter underneath.

<Aside type="tip">
The transport keys work in every Nota window, including a detached device window and the
Mixer.
</Aside>

## Launch quantize

In the [Session](../../views/session/) view, clips and scenes start on the next bar (or
another division) so they always land in time. That setting, **Quant**, lives in the Session
toolbar.
