## Kinds of track

| Track | Holds | Plays through |
|---|---|---|
| **Instrument** | MIDI clips | An instrument — built-in or a plug-in — then its effects |
| **Audio** | Audio clips | Its effects |
| **Return** | Nothing — it receives **sends** from other tracks | Its effects (a shared reverb or delay) |
| **Group** | Other tracks, folded under it | Its effects, as a submix of its tracks |
| **Master** | The sum of everything | Out to your speakers |

Return tracks and the master sit at the bottom of the Arrangement, below the scrolling tracks.

## Adding tracks

- **Double-click an instrument** in the browser — a new instrument track with that
  instrument and an empty one-bar clip.
- **Drag** an instrument, a sample or a loop from the browser onto the empty space below the
  tracks. Dropped onto an existing instrument track instead, an instrument **replaces** that
  track's instrument; a sample dropped on an audio track lands on it.
- **Right-click** the empty space below the tracks — **Add instrument track**, **Add audio
  track**, **Add return track**. A track's own menu has the same under **Add track**: the new
  track goes right after it (or inside it, if it's a group).

## The track header

<Shot id="start-here/main-window" alt="The Arrangement with the Rhythm group, Drums, Bass, Keys, Lead and Texture tracks" />

Each header carries the track's name and type, and:

- **M** mute and **S** solo,
- **●** arm — the track records when you press Record,
- the input — for an audio track the hardware input; for an instrument track **MIDI from**
  another instrument track (layering — see below),
- a **volume** fader and a **pan** bar, with the track's meter. Double-click either to reset it.

Drag a header by its name to move the track. The selected track's devices open in the panel
at the bottom.

## Selecting several tracks

<Kbd>⇧</Kbd>-click headers to build a selection (or click a header and press <Kbd>⌘ A</Kbd>).
Then <Kbd>⌘ C</Kbd> <Kbd>⌘ X</Kbd> <Kbd>⌘ V</Kbd> copy, cut and paste them, <Kbd>⌘ D</Kbd>
duplicates them and <Kbd>Delete</Kbd> removes them — each as one undo step. Right-click the
selection to group, colour or freeze all of them at once.

## Names and colours

Right-click a header ▸ **Rename…** to name a track; until you do, an instrument track is
called after its instrument and an audio track "Audio" and its number. **Color** offers nine colour families — Drums, Perc, Bass, Keys, Texture, FX,
Brass, Vox, Return — each in three shades. A track you haven't coloured takes its group's
colour, one shade per sibling, so a group reads as one family.

## Groups

Select tracks and press <Kbd>⌘ G</Kbd> to put them in a group; <Kbd>⌘ ⇧ G</Kbd> ungroups.
A group is a submix: its fader, effects, mute and solo act on all its tracks together, and
groups can sit inside groups. Its row stays slim — a titled bar with its arrow, mute, solo and
level — and opens to the full controls when you select it. Click the arrow to fold the group's
tracks away.

## Routing

- **Sends** — every track can send part of its signal to each return track: turn up its send
  in the [Mixer](../../views/mixer/) or the Session view.
- **MIDI from** — an instrument track can play the MIDI of another instrument track through
  its own instrument, to layer two sounds. It gets the notes **after** the source's MIDI
  effects, so an arpeggiator on the source drives both. Recording on the receiving track
  prints those notes into a clip.
- **Record input** — right-click ▸ **Record input** chooses what an audio track records: a
  hardware input, the master, or another track.

## Freezing a track

A heavy instrument or effect chain can be rendered to audio to free the CPU:

- **Freeze** (in the detail panel's header) renders the track and puts its devices to sleep;
  it still plays exactly the same. Right-click **Freeze** for **Flatten**, which turns the
  track into a plain audio track for good.
- **Live Freeze** bounces to a linked audio track below and puts the source to sleep. **Edit**
  wakes the source; **Done** re-freezes it.
