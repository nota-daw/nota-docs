## Kinds of track

| Track | Holds | Plays through |
|---|---|---|
| **Instrument** | MIDI clips | An instrument — built-in or a plug-in — then its effects |
| **Audio** | Audio clips | Its effects |
| **Return** | Nothing — it receives **sends** from other tracks | Its effects (a shared reverb or delay) |
| **Group** | Other tracks, folded under it | Its effects, as a submix of its tracks |
| **Master** | The sum of everything | Out to your speakers |

Return tracks and the master sit at the bottom of the Arrangement, below the scrolling tracks.
Each of these rows has a level meter and a volume fader with its value in dB — turn up a send
and watch the return's meter move, or lower a return without opening the Mixer.
Double-click a fader to reset it to 0 dB.

## Adding tracks

- **Double-click an instrument** in the browser — a new instrument track with that
  instrument and an empty one-bar clip. If an instrument track is selected, the instrument
  **replaces** that track's instead (its clips and effects stay, and Undo brings the old one
  back), so you can try several instruments on one part in a row. Hold **⌥** (Alt on Windows
  and Linux) while double-clicking to always get a new track. Racks are never replaced this
  way.
- **Drag** an instrument, a sample or a loop from the browser onto the empty space below the
  tracks. Dropped onto an existing instrument track instead, an instrument **replaces** that
  track's instrument; a sample dropped on an audio track lands on it.
- **Right-click** the empty space below the tracks — **Add instrument track**, **Add audio
  track**, **Add return track**. A track's own menu has the same under **Add track**
  (**Instrument**, **Audio**, **Return**): the new track goes right after it (or inside it,
  if it's a group).
- **Add track ▸ Recent** lists the five instruments you added most recently — built-in or
  plug-in, from the browser, a menu or an AI assistant. Click one to get a new track with that
  instrument right after the track you clicked. The list is yours, not the project's; a
  plug-in you uninstall drops out of it until you install it again.

## The track header

<Shot id="start-here/main-window" alt="The Arrangement with the Rhythm group, Drums, Bass, Keys, Lead and Texture tracks" />

Each header carries the track's name and type, and:

- **M** mute and **S** solo,
- **●** arm — the track records when you press Record, and an armed instrument track plays
  the notes from your keyboard. A click arms that track alone, and so does selecting an instrument track;
  <Kbd>⌘</Kbd>-click arms more than one,
- the input — for an audio track the hardware input; for an instrument track **MIDI from**
  another instrument track (layering — see below),
- a **volume** fader and a **pan** bar, with the track's meter. Double-click either to reset it.

Drag a header by its name to move the track. The selected track's devices open in the panel
at the bottom.

## Selecting several tracks

Click a header, then <Kbd>⇧</Kbd>-click another to select every track between them (in
either direction); <Kbd>⌘</Kbd>-click adds or removes a single track (or click a header and
press <Kbd>⌘ A</Kbd> for all of them).
Then <Kbd>⌘ C</Kbd> <Kbd>⌘ X</Kbd> <Kbd>⌘ V</Kbd> copy, cut and paste them, <Kbd>⌘ D</Kbd>
duplicates them and <Kbd>Delete</Kbd> removes them — each as one undo step. Right-click the
selection to colour, size, freeze or group all of them at once.

## The track menu: name, colour, height

Right-click a header to open the track menu. Its top line names the track and shows its type
and position (**Audio · 3**); on a selection it reads **3 tracks**.

- **Colour** — click a swatch right in the menu: nine colour families, each in a base, a light
  and a dark shade, plus **Auto**. The current colour is outlined. A track you haven't coloured
  takes its group's colour, one shade per sibling, so a group reads as one family.
- **Height** — **Compact**, **Standard** or **Large**. <Kbd>⌥</Kbd>-click (Alt on Windows and
  Linux) sizes every track at once. You can also drag a track's bottom edge.
- **Rename…** names the track; until you do, an instrument track is called after its
  instrument and an audio track "Audio" and its number.

Commands that show a current choice — **Record input**, **MIDI from** — print it on the
right, so you can read it without opening the submenu. **Delete** is always last, in red.

## Groups

Select tracks and press <Kbd>⌘ G</Kbd> to put them in a group; <Kbd>⌘ ⇧ G</Kbd> ungroups.
A group is a submix: its fader, effects, mute and solo act on all its tracks together, and
groups can sit inside groups. Its row stays slim — a titled bar with its arrow, mute, solo and
level — and opens to the full controls when you select it. Click the arrow to fold the group's
tracks away.

## Routing

- **Sends** — every track can send part of its signal to each return track: turn up its send
  in the [Mixer](../../views/mixer/) or the Session view. A new project comes with two
  returns ready to send to: **Reverb** and **Delay**, both set to **Wet Only**.
- **MIDI from** — an instrument track can play the MIDI of another instrument track through
  its own instrument, to layer two sounds. It gets the notes **after** the source's MIDI
  effects, so an arpeggiator on the source drives both. Recording on the receiving track
  prints those notes into a clip.
- **Record input** — right-click ▸ **Record input** chooses what an audio track records: a
  hardware input, the master, or another track.

## Freezing a track

A heavy instrument or effect chain can be rendered to audio to free the CPU:

- **Freeze** (in the detail panel's header) renders the track and puts its devices to sleep;
  it still plays exactly the same. Right-click **Freeze** for **Flatten**, which turns the
  track into a plain audio track for good.
- **Live freeze** bounces to a linked audio track below and puts the source to sleep. **Edit**
  wakes the source; **Done** re-freezes it. In the track menu **Live freeze** is a switch: it
  is on while the track is live-frozen, and switching it off unfreezes (wakes the source).
