Open Settings with **File ▸ Settings…**, or **Settings** on the start window.

<Shot id="preferences/audio" alt="Settings ▸ Audio" />

Which device Nota plays through and records from is on [Outputs](../outputs/) and
[Inputs](../inputs/); this page sets how it runs.

## Audio device

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Sample rate** | Device default, 44 100, 48 000, 88 200 or 96 000 Hz |
| **Buffer size** | Device default, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024 or 2048 samples |
| **Exclusive mode** (Windows) | WASAPI exclusive — the lowest latency, but other apps go silent while Nota plays |

**Latency** shows what the buffer means in milliseconds. A smaller buffer means less delay between
playing and hearing — best while recording; a larger one gives the CPU more room — best while mixing
big projects.

The status bar shows the rate and buffer that are actually running ("48 kHz · 256"). Click it
to pick another size from this same list — playback continues — or choose **Audio settings…**
to open this page. The sizes stay unavailable while you are recording. Under ASIO the driver
sets the buffer, so the menu offers **Driver settings…** instead; in WASAPI exclusive mode it
tells you the device's own buffer is in use.

## ASIO (Windows)

On Windows, **Audio system** chooses between **WASAPI** (the rows above) and **ASIO** — the
driver that came with your audio interface, for the lowest, steadiest latency. With no ASIO
driver installed, the ASIO option is greyed out; install your interface's driver, or a generic
one such as FlexASIO.

With ASIO chosen, the rows change. The driver is both output and input; its output and input
channel pairs are picked on [Outputs](../outputs/#asio-windows) and [Inputs](../inputs/#asio-windows).

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Driver** | The ASIO driver to use. Nota opens one driver at a time and lets go of it when you switch away, so other programs can use it |
| **Driver settings…** | Opens the driver's own panel. Change the buffer size there — Nota picks the change up within a couple of seconds and keeps playing |
| **Sample rate** | A request: if the driver can't run at that rate, the row goes back and says so |
| **Buffer size** | Read-only — set in the driver's settings |

**Latency** shows the driver's output latency and, when it has inputs, the round trip. Recorded
audio is moved earlier by that latency, so takes line up with what you were playing along to.

If the driver is missing, busy in another program or stops responding, Nota switches to WASAPI on
the system default device; the status bar names the driver and the reason, and **Latency** reads
**ASIO unavailable (…) → WASAPI**. Your choice stays ASIO: **Try again** (where **Driver
settings…** was) goes back to the driver once it's free.

## Test

- **Performance** — **CPU check** measures the audio thread's load for 5 seconds. Play your project
  meanwhile for a real-world figure: it reports the average, the peak and any dropouts.
