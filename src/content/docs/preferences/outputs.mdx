| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Output** | The audio device Nota plays through. **System default** follows the computer's own choice |
| **Main** · **Cue** · **Click** · **Click level** | Which outputs of that device play the master and your private Cue mix, and where the click goes — see [Output pairs](#output-pairs) |

Sample rate, buffer size and latency are on [Audio](../audio/).

## Output pairs

On an audio interface with more than two outputs, **Output pairs** splits what you hear from
what the audience hears: **Main** goes to one pair of outputs, **Cue** — your own private mix —
to another, for example Main on 1/2 for the speakers and Cue on 3/4 for your headphones.

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Main** | The output pair the audience hears — the master. **Out 1/2** unless you choose another |
| **Cue** | A second pair for your Cue mix, or **No cue output**. Main's pair can't be picked here, nor Cue's pair in Main |
| **Click** | Where the metronome and the count-in go: **Main**, **Cue** or **Both**. Without a Cue output only **Main** is available |
| **Click level** | The click's own level: **Off**, or −18 to +6 dB |

The pairs are listed by number and by the name the device gives them (**Out 3/4 · Phones**); a
lone last output is listed on its own (**Out 5**). A device with just two outputs keeps Main on
1/2 and says **No cue output — this device has 2 outputs**.

Changes apply at once, while you play — the device doesn't restart. The setup is kept for each
device on this computer, so your interface keeps Cue on 3/4 while the laptop's speakers stay a
plain stereo pair. Which tracks are in Cue, and the Cue level, are saved with the project — see
[Cue: your own mix](../../views/mixer/#cue-your-own-mix).

If the device doesn't have a saved pair any more — another device is chosen, or the interface
is unplugged — the pair is listed as **Out 3/4 — not on this device**:

- a missing **Cue** pair goes silent. Nothing is moved into Main, so the audience never hears
  your Cue mix by accident;
- a missing **Main** pair plays on outputs 1/2 instead, and says so here and on the Master strip.

## ASIO (Windows)

With **ASIO** chosen as the audio system on [Audio](../audio/#asio-windows), the driver is both
Nota's output and its input, so it is picked there, not here. This page names it, with
**Change on the Audio page** to go there. While the driver runs, **Main** under
[Output pairs](#output-pairs) is the channel pair Nota plays through, with **Cue** beside it if
you want one; the pairs are named as the driver names its channels. Outputs that carry neither
stay silent.

<Aside>
No sound with Bluetooth headphones on macOS? Pick them as the **Output** here; see also
[Troubleshooting](../../reference/troubleshooting/).
</Aside>
