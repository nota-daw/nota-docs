## Waveform

How audio clips draw on the Arrangement lanes:

| Style | Looks like |
|---|---|
| **Smooth** | The default. A fine outline of the peaks, in a lighter shade, around a denser body that follows the loudness (RMS) of the audio. Zoomed out, no peak slips between pixels; zoomed in, the wave slopes from point to point, down to a thin line at sample level |
| **Solid** | Every pixel drawn |
| **Bars** | The peaks spaced out, so the rhythm reads |

The choice applies at once to every clip and is saved. If you picked **Bars** in an earlier
version, Nota shows **Smooth** once after the update; pick **Bars** again to bring it back.

The [audio clip editor](../../editing/audio-clips/) always draws **Smooth**, whatever you pick
here. The browser's [preview player](../../library/preview-player/) follows only **Solid**:
with **Solid** it draws every pixel, and with **Smooth** or **Bars** it draws spaced bars.

## Colour themes

Pick a mode first, then a theme. Each card is a small Nota window drawn in that theme's
colours; the chosen one is framed in the accent colour.

| Mode | What Nota wears |
|---|---|
| **Dark** | The card you pick in the **Dark** row: **Studio Dark** (the default), **Ember Graphite** or one of your dark themes |
| **Light** | The card you pick in the **Light** row: **Studio Light**, **Ember Paper** or one of your light themes |
| **Follow system** | Your **In dark mode** theme while the computer is in dark mode and your **In light mode** theme while it is light, switching as soon as the computer does |

Under **Follow system** the two rows are labelled **In dark mode** and **In light mode**, and
each keeps its own pick. Nota remembers a pick per row, so going from **Dark** to **Light** and
back returns to the same theme. Clicking a card in the other row while in **Dark** or **Light**
switches the mode with it.

Every pick applies at once to every open window — and to
[Nota Remote](../../control/nota-remote/) while it follows Nota — and is saved. The built-in
themes change colours only. A theme of your own can also change the typeface and how round the
corners are; layout and sizes stay the same in every theme.

## Your own themes

A theme is a small JSON file: a name, dark or light, and one colour for each part of Nota, plus
— if you like — a typeface and corner roundness. Put one in the **themes folder** and it shows
up as a card in its row, next to the built-ins, with **User theme** under its name. Its name is
drawn in its own typeface.

<Shot id="preferences/appearance-themes" alt="Settings ▸ Appearance with two user themes, the themes folder buttons and a file that could not be loaded" />

The quickest start is a copy of a theme you like:

<Steps>

1. Right-click any card — a built-in too — and choose **Duplicate**. Nota writes a copy into the
   themes folder, named after the theme with "copy" added, and wears it.

2. Right-click the copy ▸ **Reveal in Finder** (**Show in Explorer** on Windows) and open the
   file in a text editor. Change the colours, the `name`, and add a `font` or `radius` if you
   want them — see [Theme files](../../reference/theme-files/).

3. Back in Appearance, click **Reload** under the cards. Nota reads the folder again and wears
   the changed theme at once.

</Steps>

**Open themes folder** opens the folder itself (Nota creates it if it isn't there yet):

| System | Themes folder |
|---|---|
| macOS | `~/Library/Application Support/Nota/Themes` |
| Windows | `%APPDATA%\Nota\Themes` |
| Linux | `~/.config/nota/Themes` |

Nota reads the folder when it starts, when you open Appearance and when you click **Reload**.
Files you delete or break are handled quietly:

- **A file Nota can't load** is listed under the cards, under **Some theme files could not be
  loaded**, with every reason — a missing colour, a bad hex value, an id a built-in already uses.
  It never opens a dialog, and the other themes still load.
- **A theme that is hard to read** still loads. Its card says **2 warnings** (or how many);
  hover it to see which text is too faint on which surface, or which track colours are too alike.
- **A typeface you don't have** is replaced by Nota's own Geist, with a warning on the card.
- **The theme you wear disappears** (deleted, renamed, broken): Nota wears Studio Dark — Studio
  Light while **Follow system** is in light mode — and says so above the cards. Your choice is
  kept: put the file back, click **Reload**, and Nota wears your theme again.

Your [accent colour](#accent-colour) is worn in your themes too. With the accent on **Orange**
(the default), a theme keeps its own accent colours; any other pick replaces them, as in the
built-ins.

To design a theme together with an AI assistant — it can render previews of every part of Nota
in your colours before you save — see [AI control over MCP](../../ai/mcp/#design-a-theme).

## Accent colour

The accent marks what is live or chosen: the playing Play key, the playhead, the loop,
selected clips and notes, toggles that are on, the chosen segment, menu hover, a device's
power switch and a knob while you drag it. It is orange by default; **Accent** under the
colour theme changes it.

- Click a swatch — **Orange**, **Lime**, **Aqua**, **Sky**, **Violet**, **Magenta** or
  **Pink** (hover one for its name). The chosen swatch has a ring, and its name shows at the
  end of the row.
- Or click **Custom** for your own colour. A picker opens under it: drag in the
  saturation/brightness area and along the hue strip, or type a hex value (`#4C6FFF`, or
  short `#46F`). **In themes** shows how each of the four themes will wear it; a dot marks a
  theme where Nota adjusted its lightness. Click outside the picker or press <Kbd>Return</Kbd>
  to keep the colour, <Kbd>Esc</Kbd> to go back to the accent you had.

One accent is worn in every theme, tuned to each so it stays readable: the hue is yours,
the lightness may move a little in a theme where the accent would be too faint, or the text
on it too weak. A colour that would read like the record red, the mute amber, the solo blue
or the meter green — or a grey — is not applied; the picker says why and the accent stays
as it was. Mute, solo, record, the meters, track colours and device colours never change
with the accent.

The choice applies at once to every open window and to [Nota Remote](../../control/nota-remote/),
and is saved. The theme cards above show each theme with your accent.

## Interface scale

Makes everything in Nota larger or smaller: text, buttons, knobs, track headers, device
cards, menus, tooltips and dialogs. Pick **125 %** or more when the text is hard to read —
on a laptop screen, say — or **75 %** to fit more tracks and devices on a small screen.
**100 %** is the default.

The scale applies at once to every open window and is saved for the next launch. Open
windows keep their size on screen, so their contents get more or less room (dialogs you
can't resize grow and shrink with the scale); windows you
open afterwards start at a matching size. At a large scale on a small screen some panels
are tighter than usual.

The scale is on top of the operating system's own display scaling, and the timeline, piano
roll and audio editor still zoom their content as before. Plug-in windows are drawn by the
plug-in and keep their own size.

## Language

Sets the language of Nota's menus, panels, dialogs, tooltips, status and error messages and the
command palette. Nota comes in **English**; every other language is downloaded from
[nota-translations](https://github.com/nota-daw/nota-translations), where translators keep them.
The list shows English, the languages you have, and — when Nota is online — the ones you can
download, each in its own name with how much of this version it covers (**99 %**: a few new texts
still show in English).

Pick a language you don't have and Nota downloads it right there (a small bar shows the
progress), checks it against its published checksum and keeps it, so it works offline from then
on. If the download fails the row says why and nothing changes. When a better translation of your
language is published, an **Update** button appears beside the list; **Remove** deletes the
chosen language (Nota goes back to English from the next start).

The choice is saved and applies the next time Nota starts. Right after you pick one the row says
**Applies after restart**, with a small **Restart** button beside it: it closes Nota the usual way
(asking first about unsaved changes — cancel that and Nota stays open) and opens it again in the
new language. Pick the language that is running again and the note goes away. A copy of Nota that
can't start itself again (a development build) says **Applies the next time Nota starts**
instead.

If you used French before Nota started downloading languages, it fetches French in the
background on its first start, still in English, and the status bar says **Français is ready —
restart to use it**; click it to restart. With **Check for updates automatically** off (Settings ▸
[Updates](../updates/)) Nota never goes online by itself: the row offers **Show available
languages**, and you download a language by choosing it.

**Load file…** is for translators: pick a language file from your disk and Nota runs in it after
a restart, reading it from that place each time, so you can edit and restart to see your changes.
The entries it can't use are listed under the row (and those show in English). How to write and
publish a translation is in the nota-translations README.

What the language doesn't touch:

- **Device and parameter names** — "Cutoff", "Attack", "Reverb", "Nota Volt" — stay in English
  in every language, as in studio vocabulary and tutorials. The tooltips and descriptions
  around them are translated.
- **Your own names** for projects, tracks, clips, scenes and sections are never translated, and
  the language never changes what is saved in a project.
- [Nota Remote](../../control/nota-remote/), the [AI control](../../ai/mcp/) tool descriptions and
  the log files are not translated.

On a Mac, the system's own text — the application menu's **Hide** and **Quit**, the open and
save panels — follows the language too, from the next start. English is the default whatever
your operating system's language is: nothing changes until you choose. A text that has no
translation yet shows in English.
