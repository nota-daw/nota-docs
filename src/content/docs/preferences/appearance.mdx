| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Colour theme** | **Dark**, **Light** or **Follow system**, then a theme from the cards below — see [Colour themes](#colour-themes) |
| **Waveform** | How audio clips draw in the Arrangement, and samples in the browser's preview: **Bars** spaces the peaks out so the rhythm reads; **Solid** draws every pixel |
| **Interface scale** | **75 %**, **100 %**, **125 %**, **150 %** or **200 %** — the size of the whole interface. See [Interface scale](#interface-scale) |
| **AI control (MCP)** | Let an AI assistant work in Nota — the switch, the port, the status and **Copy config**. See [AI control over MCP](../../ai/mcp/) |

## Colour themes

Pick a mode first, then a theme. Each card is a small Nota window drawn in that theme's
colours; the chosen one is framed in the accent colour.

| Mode | What Nota wears |
|---|---|
| **Dark** | The card you pick in the **Dark** row: **Studio Dark** (the default) or **Ember Graphite** |
| **Light** | The card you pick in the **Light** row: **Studio Light** or **Ember Paper** |
| **Follow system** | Your **In dark mode** theme while the computer is in dark mode and your **In light mode** theme while it is light, switching as soon as the computer does |

Under **Follow system** the two rows are labelled **In dark mode** and **In light mode**, and
each keeps its own pick. Nota remembers a pick per row, so going from **Dark** to **Light** and
back returns to the same theme. Clicking a card in the other row while in **Dark** or **Light**
switches the mode with it.

Every pick applies at once to every open window — and to
[Nota Remote](../../control/nota-remote/) while it follows Nota — and is saved. Themes change colours only — layout
and sizes stay the same.

## Interface scale

Makes everything in Nota larger or smaller: text, buttons, knobs, track headers, device
cards, menus, tooltips and dialogs. Pick **125 %** or more when the text is hard to read —
on a laptop screen, say — or **75 %** to fit more tracks and devices on a small screen.
**100 %** is the default.

The scale applies at once to every open window and is saved for the next launch. Open
windows keep their size on screen, so their contents get more or less room (dialogs you
can't resize grow and shrink with the scale); windows you
open afterwards start at a matching size. At a large scale on a small screen some panels
are tighter than usual.

The scale is on top of the operating system's own display scaling, and the timeline, piano
roll and audio editor still zoom their content as before. Plug-in windows are drawn by the
plug-in and keep their own size.
