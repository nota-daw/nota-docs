Automation makes a value move over time — a filter opening up, a fade, a delay swelling into a
drop. Track volume and pan can be automated, and so can every parameter of a built-in device,
plug-in parameters and MIDI effects. On the master you automate its volume, pan, the devices
on the master chain — and the song's **tempo**. Sends can't be automated: they have no lane.

<Shot id="editing/automation-lanes" alt="Automation mode in the Arrangement: the Lead track's volume lane with a curve across the song" />

## Showing the lanes

<At id="ui:transport.automation" /> Click the **automation** button in the transport bar (<Kbd>⌘ ⇧ A</Kbd>). Each track's lane shows
one parameter, picked with the pill at the start of the lane — click it and choose from the
list. Parameters that already carry automation are marked with a dot and listed first.

While automation mode is on, touching any knob or fader of a track — on a device, a plug-in or
the mixer — switches that track's lane to it.

Return tracks have lanes too, in their rows pinned under the tracks: volume, pan and the
parameters of the devices on the return. They edit exactly like a track's lane. The **Master**
row below them has the same pill: **Volume**, **Tempo**, **Pan**, then a submenu for each device
on the master chain. Touching a master device's knob in automation mode shows its lane there.

## Drawing

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| <At id="ui:arrangement" /> Double-click the lane | Add a point (also between two points) |
| Drag a point | Move it |
| Double-click a point | Delete it |
| <Kbd>⌥</Kbd>-drag a segment | Bend it into a curve |
| <Kbd>⇧</Kbd>-drag | Select a range of the lane |
| <Kbd>⌘ C</Kbd> <Kbd>⌘ X</Kbd> <Kbd>⌘ V</Kbd> · <Kbd>⌘ D</Kbd> | Copy, cut, paste · duplicate the selected range |

A single click moves the playhead, as anywhere else in the Arrangement. Right-click the lane
for **Copy**, **Cut**, **Paste** and **Delete automation**.

## Recording automation

<At id="ui:transport.record" /> There are no modes to set. Press **Record** and move a control: its lane records what you do.
A control you move with the mouse stops writing when you let go; a hardware knob, fader, stick
or trigger keeps writing its last value until the transport stops. Sends record the same way —
move a send knob in a track header, the Mixer or the **Sends** card.

## Taking over by hand

Automation always plays back. If you move an automated control **while not recording**, your
hand wins: that lane is overridden and stops pulling the knob back. The button next to the
automation button lights up while anything is overridden — click it to hand every lane back to
playback.

## Tempo

Tempo changes are automation on the master: pick **Tempo** in the Master row's pill and draw
the tempo like any other lane. The lane reads in BPM:

<Shot id="editing/automation-tempo" alt="The Master row's Tempo lane: a steady tempo easing down into a ritardando, then an instant jump to a faster tempo; the bars above widen through the slow-down" />

- a **curve** between two points is a smooth accelerando or ritardando — bend it with
  <Kbd>⌥</Kbd>-drag;
- **two points on one beat** are an instant tempo change;
- the numbers at the lane's right edge are the highest and lowest tempo it shows — click one
  to change it. Tempo itself stays within 20–300 BPM.

Playback, the grid, the metronome, MIDI, warped audio, freezing, bouncing and export all follow
the drawn tempo, and you hear an edit while the song plays. Audio that isn't warped keeps its
place in time. While the Tempo lane has points the transport's **BPM** shows the tempo under
the playhead and can't be typed into — delete the points to set one fixed tempo again.

Moving or deleting a section of the song, or inserting time, leaves the tempo where it is.
Projects made with the earlier tempo track open with the same timing, their tempo moved onto
this lane.

## Automation moves with the clips

When you move clips, the automation under them moves along. Moved to **another track**, a clip
takes only its Volume and Pan automation — device automation stays on the track it was written
for. **Edit ▸ Lock envelopes** pins all automation to the timeline instead, so you can rearrange
clips without dragging it along.

## Other kinds of envelope

- The **master volume** is the Master row's **Volume** target; its pan and tempo are
  targets there too (see above).
- A **MIDI clip** has Velocity and Volume envelopes inside the clip — see
  [Piano roll](../piano-roll/#envelopes).
- An **audio clip** has Volume and Pan envelopes and an ADSR — see
  [Audio clips](../audio-clips/#envelope).
- To move a knob with a track's loudness instead of drawn points (a peak controller, or ducking
  one track under another), patch an **Envelope Follower** in Modular — see
  [Move a knob with another track's loudness](../../views/modular/#move-a-knob-with-another-tracks-loudness).

<Aside type="tip">
Lanes follow their device: reorder or remove devices and the automation stays with the
parameter it belongs to.
</Aside>
