## Record into the Arrangement

<Steps>

1. **Arm** the track: click the **●** button in its header. An audio track records audio, an
   instrument track records MIDI.

2. Choose what it records. For an **audio track**, right-click the header ▸ **Record input**:
   the hardware input, the master, or another track. An **instrument track** records the notes
   you play on a MIDI keyboard, the computer keyboard, a gamepad or your phone.

3. Put the playhead where the take should start and press **Record** (<Kbd>⌘ R</Kbd>). Press
   <Kbd>Space</Kbd> or **Stop** to finish.

</Steps>

A click on **●** arms that track and disarms the others, so it's quick to pick the instrument you
play. <Kbd>⌘</Kbd>-click (<Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>-click on Windows and Linux) arms a track and keeps the
others armed — while recording, a click never disarms another track. Selecting an instrument
track, or adding one, arms it the same way, so the keyboard plays the track you're on; that
leaves the arms alone when several tracks or an audio track are armed. Arm an instrument track
and an audio track together and one take records both — the MIDI and
the audio. Nota records the first armed track of each kind.

Disarming a track while it records ends its take.

A click on **●** arms that track alone and disarms the others, so picking the instrument the
keyboard plays is one click. <Kbd>⌘</Kbd>-click (<Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>-click on Windows and Linux)
arms several tracks. While recording, a click never disarms another track.

To have the selection pick the track, turn on **Transport ▸ Auto-arm selected track**: then
selecting an instrument track arms it, unless several tracks or an audio track are armed. It
is off by default.

While a MIDI take records, each note appears the moment you press the key and grows with the
playhead for as long as you hold it; a new note flashes in the accent colour as it lands. The
same live notes show in the [piano roll](../piano-roll/) when it has the take open, and in a
Session slot being recorded.

## Count-in

Right-click the metronome for a **count-in** of 1, 2 or 4 bars. Record from stop then counts in
first; the clicks are never part of the take. See [Transport](../../basics/transport/#metronome-and-count-in).

Notes you play during the count-in are recorded — a pickup into the first bar lands exactly
where you played it, and the take's clip starts earlier to hold it. The same goes for a key
you are already holding when recording starts. A take that starts at 1.1 can't begin earlier,
so its clip starts at 1.1 and the part of the note before it is kept out of sight: move the
clip right and drag its left edge out to hear it.

## Mono or stereo input

The hardware input is listed three times — in **Record input**, the header's input box and the
Session mixer's **I/O** row:

- **Ext 1** — input 1 alone, a mono source such as a mic. It's recorded and heard in the middle,
  on both sides.
- **Ext 2** — input 2 alone, the same way.
- **Ext 1/2** — both inputs as a stereo pair: a stereo synth, a stereo mic pair, a mixer's output.

A new audio track takes **Ext 1**, since most mics are mono. With a mic plugged into input 1 of a
two-input interface, **Ext 1/2** would record it on the left only. Projects made before 0.58 keep
the stereo pair.

## Hearing the input

The headphones button next to an audio track's input turns on **monitoring**: you hear the
input live through the track's effects and fader, whether or not the transport is playing.
While monitoring is on, the track plays its input instead of its clips. The master can't be
monitored — it would feed back.

## Recording over a loop

With the loop on, **audio** recording fills the loop range: each time round, the new pass
replaces the previous one, so you can keep playing until you get it, then stop. Turning the
loop on in the middle of a take starts recording the loop region from there.

For **MIDI**, the loop-record button next to **Loop** in the transport chooses what a new pass does
(<Kbd>⌘ ⇧ R</Kbd> or **Transport → Loop record: overdub / replace** switch it too):

- **Overdub** — every pass adds to the take. Build a beat in layers: kick on the first pass,
  snare on the next, hats after that.
- **Replace** — the first note you play on a new pass clears what the earlier passes of this
  take recorded, so only your latest pass remains. A pass you don't play on leaves the take as
  it is, and notes that were in the clip before you hit record always stay.

A switch takes effect from the next pass, and Nota remembers your choice. Session slots keep
their own overdub recording.

A key you hold across the loop end keeps sounding, and in the take it becomes two notes: one up
to the loop end and one from the loop start.

## Recording from another track

An audio track's **Record input** can be another track, a group or a return — handy for
printing a performance through its effects, or a submix, to audio. Nota keeps it sample-aligned.

## Recording into Session

In the [Session view](../../views/session/#recording-into-a-slot), arm a track, select a scene
and click **Session rec** — the take goes into that track's slot. Click again to keep it (cut
to whole bars), or set **Fixed** to 1–8 bars and it stops by itself. An empty slot's
**Record here** does the same for that slot. A track plays one slot at a time, so a take
stops the clip that track was playing. From stop, with a count-in set, the slot blinks
**Rec in…** through the count-in and the take starts as it ends; a note you hold into the take
starts with it. Stop during the count-in leaves the slot empty.

## Recording automation

**Record** also records automation: while it's on, move any knob or fader and that parameter's
lane writes what you do. See [Automation](../automation/#recording-automation).

<Aside type="tip">
Latency matters most when recording. Pick a small buffer in
[Settings ▸ Audio](../../preferences/audio/) while you record and a larger one while you mix.
</Aside>
