Double-click a MIDI clip — in the Arrangement or a Session slot — and it opens in the **Clip**
tab of the panel at the bottom.

<Shot id="start-here/clip-editor" alt="A MIDI clip in the clip editor: clip settings on the left, notes and velocity stems on the right" />

## Notes

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Double-click empty grid | Add a note, with the length and velocity of the note you last clicked, moved, resized or changed the velocity of |
| Double-click a note | Delete it |
| Drag a note | Move it in time and pitch |
| Drag with <Kbd>⌥</Kbd> | Ignore the grid for this drag |
| Drag a note's edge | Change where it starts or ends |
| Click a note · <Kbd>⇧</Kbd>-click | Select it · add it to the selection |
| Drag across empty grid | Select the notes in the rectangle |
| <Kbd>⌘ A</Kbd> | Select all notes |
| <Kbd>←</Kbd> <Kbd>→</Kbd> | Move the selection by the grid |
| <Kbd>↑</Kbd> <Kbd>↓</Kbd> · <Kbd>⇧ ↑</Kbd> <Kbd>⇧ ↓</Kbd> | Transpose by a semitone · an octave |
| <Kbd>⌘ D</Kbd> | Duplicate the selection right after itself (replacing the notes it lands on) |
| <Kbd>⌘ C</Kbd> <Kbd>⌘ X</Kbd> <Kbd>⌘ V</Kbd> | Copy, cut, paste notes |
| <Kbd>Delete</Kbd> | Delete the selected notes |
| Click the ruler · drag along it | Move the playhead there · scrub, snapped to the grid |

Edits play straight away — drag a note while the loop runs and you hear it move. The playhead
shows in the piano roll whether the transport is playing or stopped, so you can see where
playback starts.

## Hearing notes

With the **speaker** switch in the clip editor's header on (it is by default), a note plays on
the track's instrument while you place it, click it or drag it, and plays again at each new
pitch as you drag — so you can find a pitch by ear without starting playback. Turn the switch
off to edit in silence; Nota remembers it. Nothing plays this way while a take is recording.

The keys on the left are a keyboard too, whatever the switch says:

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Click a key | Play it until you let go |
| <Kbd>⇧</Kbd>-drag along the keys | Glide through them, playing each one |
| <Kbd>⌘</Kbd>-click a key · <Kbd>⌘ ⇧</Kbd>-click | Select every note of that pitch · add them to the selection |
| Drag up or down on the keys | Zoom the rows (the key stops sounding) |

## Recorded MPE

A note recorded from an [MPE controller](../../control/midi/#pitch-wheel-aftertouch-and-mpe) or Nota
Remote's **Expressive** keys carries its expression: a line over the note traces the pitch it played
(a row per semitone) and a shade rising from its floor shows the pressure. Move, copy, duplicate,
transpose or quantize the note and its expression goes along.

### Editing it

The lane under the notes switches with the labels on its left: **Vel** (velocity), **Bend**, **Pres**
(pressure) and **Slide**. In an MPE lane every note's curve is drawn over the note's own span; the
selected notes' curves are the ones you edit, with their points marked.

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| Drag across a note | Draw its curve over the stretch you drag (the highest note there gets selected if none is) |
| <Kbd>⌥</Kbd>-drag | Draw a straight line instead |
| Drag a point | Move it in time and value |
| Double-click | Add a point · on a point, delete it |
| Right-click | **Clear** this lane's curve or **Clear All Expression** on the selected notes |

**Bend** is in semitones and scales itself to what is there (±2, 12, 24, 48 or 96); **Pres** and
**Slide** run 0–100 %, slide resting in the middle. Every gesture is one undo step and plays back
straight away. Drawn expression works on any note — no MPE controller needed — and plays on the synths
that take [MPE](../../control/midi/#pitch-wheel-aftertouch-and-mpe).

## Velocity

The lane under the notes shows each note's velocity as a stem. Drag a stem to set it; with
several notes selected, they move together. <Kbd>⇧</Kbd> adds a note to the selection.

## Several clips at once

To edit notes across clips — a bass line split over several clips, or a melody you write
against the chords of another track — select two or more MIDI clips in the Arrangement (on
one track or several) and double-click one of them. Right-click → **Edit selected clips
together**, or the same command in the command palette, does it too.

<Shot id="editing/multi-clip-editor" alt="Two bass clips and two chord clips open together, the clip strip under the ruler showing the active bass clip" />

The notes of every open clip sit on the Arrangement's bars, each in its track's colour. The
clip you double-clicked is the **active** clip: its notes are brightest, the header and the
left column show it, **Envelopes** edits it, and new notes go into it. The other clips' notes
are quieter but just as editable.

- Select, move, transpose, quantize, delete or run a **Tool** across clips — each note stays
  in its own clip, and one undo reverts the whole edit.
- Drag a note past the seam into the next open clip of its track and it moves into that
  clip. A gap between clips, or a clip that isn't open, stops it at the edge.
- The **clip strip** under the ruler shows the open clips. Click one to make it active (a
  click on a note of another clip does the same); click its lock to keep it as a dimmed
  **reference** that you can see and hear but not change. The active clip can't be locked.
- While the **Clip** tab is open it follows the Arrangement: select more clips and they
  appear together, select one and it shows on its own.
- <Kbd>⌘ D</Kbd> copies that run past a clip's end carry on into the next open clip;
  copies with no clip to land in are left out.

## Zoom

| Do this | To |
|---|---|
| <Kbd>⌘</Kbd> + wheel | Zoom in time, toward the pointer |
| <Kbd>⇧</Kbd> + wheel | Scroll in time |
| <Kbd>⌘ ⌥</Kbd> + wheel | Make the rows taller or shorter |
| Drag up or down on the keys | Taller (down) or shorter (up) rows |
| <Kbd>⌥</Kbd> + pinch on a trackpad | Taller or shorter rows |
| <Kbd>⇧ F</Kbd> or **Fit** in the header | Fit to notes |

Rows run from 6 to 36 pixels high. Zoom in to hit a note precisely, or out to see the whole
keyboard: at the smallest, all 88 keys fit on screen. The pitch under the pointer stays where
it is while you zoom. **Fit to notes** — also in the command palette — sizes and scrolls the
rows so every note in the clip is in view. On Windows and Linux use <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd> for
<Kbd>⌘</Kbd> and <Kbd>Alt</Kbd> for <Kbd>⌥</Kbd>.

Each clip remembers its own zoom and scroll; a clip you haven't opened yet starts at the
standard row height, centred on its notes.

## The left column

- **Clip** — **Start** and **Length**, and **Loop** with the loop length.
- **Quantize** — its own step (1/32 to 1/2, triplets too; 1/16 by default), which doesn't
  change with the zoom. **Quantize** pulls the notes onto it, as far as **Strength** says —
  100 % is rigid, less keeps some of the feel. The clip tools use the same step.

Notes snap to the piano roll's [snap grid](../../basics/transport/#the-snap-grid): the chip
in the clip editor's header shows the step (**Auto · 1/16**) and opens the grid menu. In
**Auto** it follows the zoom, like the Arrangement's, but the two have separate settings (the
audio clip editor shares the piano roll's).
The arrows move the selection by that step.
- **Transpose** — move the selection by semitones or octaves.

## Scale

Turn **Scale** on in the toolbar and pick a root and a scale: the rows outside it are dimmed,
so the notes that fit stand out. **Remove out of scale** deletes every note that doesn't fit.
The setting stays as you move between clips. The [Nota Scale](../../devices/midi-effects/scale/)
MIDI effect uses the same scales to correct notes live.

## Envelopes

**Envelopes** (next to **Notes**) draws curves across the clip: **Velocity** scales the notes'
velocity, **Volume** the instrument's output while the clip plays. Click to add a point, drag
it, right-click it to delete it.

## Tools

**Tools** opens generators and transforms that write notes for you. Pick a tool, set its
parameters, and **Apply** — **Add** puts the result on top of the clip's notes, **Replace**
swaps them; **Reroll** tries another **Seed**, **Revert** goes back.

**Generators** — for an empty clip:

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Rhythm** | Lays down a pattern that leans on the strong beats, as densely as you ask |
| **Seed** | Learns the clip's intervals and rhythm, then writes a new take in the same voice |
| **Stacks** | Grows every note into a chord, voiced the way you'd play it |
| **Euclidean** | Spreads a number of hits as evenly as a number of steps allows |
| **Melodic Steps** | A step sequencer in scale degrees — pick a contour and a span, get a line |
| **Shape** | Traces a curve across the clip and drops a note wherever it passes a step |

**Transforms** — for notes you already have:

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Arpeggiate** | Breaks chords into a running sequence |
| **Connect** | Fills the gaps between notes with runs that walk to the next pitch |
| **Ornament** | Decorates notes with grace notes, trills and rolls |
| **Quantize** | Pulls notes onto the grid, with swing and a strength short of rigid |
| **Recombine** | Pulls pitch and rhythm apart and puts them back together differently |
| **Span** | Rewrites note lengths — from staccato through legato to overlapping |
| **Strum** | Rolls the notes of a chord out in time instead of hitting them together |
| **Time Warp** | Bends the time axis so a phrase accelerates, drags or breathes |
| **Velocity Shaper** | Draws a curve through the velocities instead of editing them one by one |

<Aside type="tip">
A clip on a Drum Rack track also has a **Pattern** tab: a step grid with a row per pad.
</Aside>
