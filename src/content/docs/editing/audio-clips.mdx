Audio gets into a project by dragging files from the browser or your file manager, with
**File ▸ Import audio…**, or by [recording](../recording/). Double-click an audio clip — in the
Arrangement or a Session slot — to open it in the clip editor.

<Shot id="editing/audio-clip-editor" alt="An audio clip in the clip editor: Sample tab with the waveform, clip start and length, gain and pitch" />

## Tempo and key when you drop a file

Nota analyses every file it imports. A **loop** (or a long take with a clear tempo) is warped
to the project tempo as it lands, and the status line says so — "loop warped 124 → 120 BPM".
With **Transpose to project key** on (the Files tab's ⋮ menu), it is also moved to the
[project key](../../basics/transport/#tempo-time-signature-and-key) the shorter way round. A
**one-shot** — a kick, a hit — is never warped.

Long files decode in the background: the track appears straight away and its waveform fills in.

## Sample

The **Sample** tab shows the waveform with the part of the file the clip plays.

- **Clip** — **Start** and **Length**. Drag the brackets on the waveform to trim; with **Snap**
  on, they land on the beat grid.
- **Playback** — **Gain** (drag or double-click to type) and **Pitch** in semitones.
  **Reverse** plays the clip backwards.

## Warp

**Warp** stretches the audio to the project tempo without changing its pitch, and keeps it in
time when you change the tempo later. Choose how it stretches:

| Mode | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Beats** | Drums and percussive loops — keeps the hits sharp |
| **Tones** | Monophonic, pitched material — vocals, bass, leads |
| **Texture** | Pads, ambiences, noisy sounds |
| **Re-Pitch** | Like tape: speed and pitch change together |
| **Complex** | Full mixes and dense material |
| **Complex Pro** | Full mixes, with more care for formants |

**Detect tempo** reads the file's tempo and conforms it to the project. For finer control, use
**markers**: double-click the waveform to pin a marker, then drag it to line that moment up with
the grid. **To transients** pins a marker on every hit, so a drum loop locks to the grid.

## Envelope

**Envelope** draws **Volume** and **Pan** curves across the clip. Click to add a point, drag
to move it, right-click to delete it.

## ADSR

**ADSR** shapes the clip's start and end: **Attack** fades it in, **Decay** and **Sustain** set
how it settles, **Release** fades it out. You can also shape it right on the timeline: hover an
audio clip and drag the handles in its top corners (or the dot that appears). Double-click a
handle to reset that stage.

## On the timeline

Right-click an audio clip for **Reverse**, **Separate Stems…** (split it into drums, bass,
vocals and the rest — see [Separate Stems](../../ai/separate-stems/)) and **Convert** (turn its
melody, harmony or drums into MIDI — see [Convert audio to MIDI](../midi-tools/)).

Drag an audio clip onto a track with a Sampler, Nota Grain, Drum Rack or Instrument Rack and its
audio loads there; drag it out of the Arrangement and it becomes a sample you can drop anywhere —
see [Copy, paste and drag](../clipboard/#audio-clips-into-instruments).

<Aside type="tip">
A clip plays only the part between its brackets, but the whole file stays in the project — trim
generously, you can always open it back up.
</Aside>
