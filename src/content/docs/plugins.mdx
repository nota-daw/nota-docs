Nota hosts **VST3** plug-ins on macOS, Windows and Linux, and **AU** (Audio Units) on macOS —
instruments and effects alike. They sit in a track's chain or inside a rack like Nota's own
devices.

## Adding a plug-in

Scanned plug-ins appear in the browser's **Instruments** and **Audio effects** tabs, under
**PLUG-INS**, after Nota's built-in devices. The tag at the right edge names the format and vendor,
which tells the AU and VST3 builds of one plug-in apart. Double-click or drag one in, exactly as with
a built-in device.

Not there? Check [Scanning plug-ins](scanning/), or install free open-source plug-ins from
[Settings ▸ Downloads](get-plugins/).

## The plug-in card

The card comes in two sizes:

- **Small** — vendor, format, parameter count and latency, with **Open editor** and **Save preset**.
- **Full** — every parameter as a knob, with a filter and pages, showing the plug-in's own value text
  (double-click a knob for its default), and a **sidechain** source when the plug-in has one.

**Open editor** opens the plug-in's own window — on top and focused; it closes when the device is
removed. While it has focus, the computer keyboard still plays notes and the transport keys still
work.

## What Nota takes care of

- **State** — every plug-in's settings are saved inside the project.
- **Latency** — plug-in delay compensation keeps tracks aligned.
- **Tempo and position** — plug-ins get tempo, position, play state and loop points, so synced
  delays, LFOs, arpeggiators and loopers follow the song.
- **Automation and MIDI Learn** — plug-in parameters can be [automated](../editing/automation/) and
  [mapped to a controller](../control/midi-learn/).
- **Bypass** — the switch in the card header, as on any device.

## Opening a project with missing plug-ins

If a project uses plug-ins that aren't on this computer, Nota opens it anyway and says what was
skipped. If the [Nota plug-in registry](get-plugins/) has them, Nota offers to install them and
reopen the project, so they come back with their saved settings. Plug-ins are found even when they're
installed in a different folder than on the computer that saved the project.

<Aside>
Plug-ins are scanned in a separate process, so a plug-in that crashes during a scan can't take Nota
down with it.
</Aside>
