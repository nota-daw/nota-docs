Every rack — [Instrument Rack](../instrument-rack/), [Drum Rack](../drum-rack/),
[Audio Effect Rack](../audio-effect-rack/) and [MIDI Effect Rack](../midi-effect-rack/) — and [Nota Rhythm](../../instruments/rhythm/) has **eight
macros**: named knobs that drive any parameters inside, so one knob can open every snare and clap
reverb at once, or morph a whole layered patch.

## On the Instrument, Audio Effect and MIDI Effect racks

Each macro is a small knob with its name, its value (— when it drives nothing) and, in teal, how many
targets it has. Drag it up or down; double-click resets it to 0.

<Shot id="devices/racks/instrument-rack/mapping" alt="Mapping Macro 1 in place: every parameter on the chain is outlined in teal" />

**Map in place:** pick a macro and press **Map**. The card switches to **Chains**, and every parameter
on the chosen chain's devices gets a teal outline — click one to map it to the macro, click again to
unmap it. A parameter belongs to one macro: mapping it again moves it. Choose another chain to map
its parameters too; press **Map** again or <Kbd>Esc</Kbd> to finish. A mapped parameter shows its
macro's tag (**M1** …) and follows the macro; click it to open the macro.

<Shot id="devices/racks/instrument-rack/macros" alt="The Macros tab: Bright, its curve (Mixed) and two targets with their ranges" />

**The Macros tab** shows the chosen macro: double-click its name to rename it, pick its **Curve**
(**Lin**, **Exp**, **Log**, **S**) and drag each target's range — the teal band runs from min to max,
and min above max turns the response upside down. When a project gave the targets different curves,
the curve reads **Mixed** until you pick one for all. **×** removes a target.

In the Audio Effect and MIDI Effect racks the **selector** can be a target too: right-click the
selector on the Select / Zones tab, or choose **Map to the selector** in a macro's right-click menu.

## On the Drum Rack and Nota Rhythm

Each macro shows its value, its name, and — in teal — where it goes ("→ Reverb · Snare,
Clap"). An unassigned macro is an empty, dashed cell.

### Mapping

1. Select a macro.
2. Press **Map** and pick the target: a pad's own controls (Volume, Pan, Tune, Decay), a parameter of a
   chain's instrument, or of any of its effects.
3. Repeat to add more receivers.

The selected macro's **receivers** are listed with their range as a teal band and the current value
as a tick — drag the band's ends to set the range each receiver covers.

Right-click a macro to see its receivers and ranges, **Rename** it, or **Clear** it. Factory kits load
with their own macros (Tune, Decay, Hats, Drive / Tape, Room, Echo, 808 / Sub — as the kit has them).

## Controlling macros

Macros are parameters like any other: automate them, map them to a controller with
[MIDI Learn](../../../control/midi-learn/), or play them from your phone's **Macros** screen in
[Nota Remote](../../../control/nota-remote/).
