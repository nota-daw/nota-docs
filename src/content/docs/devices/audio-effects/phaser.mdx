Nota Phaser shifts the phase of a sound through a chain of all-pass stages and mixes it back with the
original: notches appear and an LFO sweeps them — the swirl of a classic phaser pedal or a rotary
speaker.

<Shot id="devices/audio-effects/phaser/card" alt="Nota Phaser: the LFO, the all-pass response and the stages" />

## LFO

Sine, triangle or saw; **Hz** (free, 0.02–8 Hz) or **Sync** (4 bars to 1/16, with dotted and
triplets); **Rate**; **Stereo** — the right channel's LFO runs 0–180° behind the left.

## The window

The live all-pass response of L and R with the notches marked — drag left and right for **Center**,
up and down for **Feedback** — and the corner frequency of both channels over two cycles.

## Stages

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Stages** | 2, 4, 6, 8 or 12 — more stages, more notches (half as many as stages) |
| **Center** | Centre of the sweep, 50 Hz–5 kHz |
| **Depth** | How far it sweeps, up to ±3 octaves |
| **Feedback** | Bipolar: positive deepens and sharpens the notches; negative moves and widens them |
| **Mix** | 0 dry · 50 % the deepest notches · 100 % wet only (phase shift alone) |
