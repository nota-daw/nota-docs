Nota Lens shows what a sound is made of. It changes nothing — put it anywhere in a chain, or on the
master, to look at the spectrum, the waveform or how the sound evolves over time.

<Shot id="devices/audio-effects/lens/card" alt="Nota Lens, Spectrum view" />

**Layout:** the **Scale** rail on the left, one graph in the middle that fills its panel, and the
controls on the right. The middle has three views: **Spectrum**, **Scope** and **Waterfall**. The
right panel has two tabs. The first is named after the view you're on and holds that view's
controls; the second, **Display**, sets how the graph is drawn. Right of the view tabs sit a live
reading, **Freeze** and the button that opens a bigger window. The reading gives the frequency, note
and level under the pointer — on Spectrum also the FFT bin number; with the pointer away, the peak
(Spectrum, Waterfall), the period with the peak-to-peak level and the RMS beside it (Scope) or the
cursor measurement.

| View | First right tab | Display |
|---|---|---|
| Spectrum | FFT, Window, Smooth, Decay, Tilt, Floor, Avg, Peak, Peak hold, Mid / Side | Source, Top, Range, Rate, Log freq, Note grid |
| Scope | Sync, Cursors, Trig or Span, Edge, Level, Holdoff, Source, Key, Key gain, Layout | Afterglow, Persist, Traces, Bright, Rate, Mid / Side |
| Waterfall | Speed, Window, FFT, Overlap, Avg, Gain, Floor, Contrast, Mid / Side | Source, Top, Range, Rate, Log freq, Note grid |

With **Cursors** on, the Scope tab shows the cursor controls instead: A and B positions, **Snap to
peaks**, **Re-arm** and **Export WAV**.

## Spectrum

An FFT spectrum on a log frequency scale: **FFT** size and window, **Avg**, smoothing, decay, a
**Tilt** (so a balanced mix looks flat), a floor, and **peak hold**. **Mid/Side** shows the mid or the
side instead of left / right. Next to the graph's title: the FFT window length and the crest factor
(**window** … ms · **crest** … dB).

## Scope

<Shot id="devices/audio-effects/lens/scope" alt="Nota Lens, Scope view" />

A triggered oscilloscope that behaves like a bench instrument: the trace is pinned to the trigger
point and stands still, older passes fade (**Afterglow**). **Trig** picks **Auto** (runs free when
nothing triggers), **Normal** or **Single**, which holds one sweep until you press **Re-arm**.
**Cursors** A and B measure time and level between two points; the reading gives Δt with its
1/Δt in Hz and the note, and ΔV. **Export WAV** saves the captured window. Next to the graph's
title: the capture state — **held**, **stable** or **free-run** — with the holdoff and the trigger
edge.

### Tempo sync

Turn on **Sync** on the Scope tab to line it up with the song instead of a trigger. Pick a **Span**:
¼ beat, ½ beat, 1 beat, 2 beats, 1 bar, 2 bars or 4 bars (a bar follows the time signature). While
the song plays, the scope shows that stretch of the beat grid: a 1 bar span starts on a bar line and
ends on the next one. The **Trig** row turns into **Span** while Sync is on. Beat lines are drawn
across it, bar lines stronger, and the labels underneath give the bar and beat at each edge.

A sweep line follows the playhead. Left of it is the pass being drawn now; right of it, the previous
pass fades out, so you always see a full span. This makes it easy to see whether a note's attack sits
on the beat or just after it, bar after bar.

- Long spans are drawn from a peak history, so a click or a transient keeps its full height even at 4
  bars.
- A seek or a loop wrap starts the pass again at the new position, instead of
  drawing old audio under the new beat lines.
- Stopped, the scope rolls freely on a track that still sounds (an instrument you play, or a
  monitored input), with **free** in the corner. A stopped audio track is silent, so its scope keeps
  the last pass.
- With Sync on, Edge, Level and Holdoff rest. **Freeze** holds both passes as they are.

### Key overlay

Lens takes a sidechain key, like the Compressor. In **Key** on the Scope tab, pick another
track: its audio (after its effects) is drawn in teal over this track's trace, in the synced scope
and in the triggered one, where it follows the same trigger and time base. The switch next to it
hides the key's trace.

- **Key Gain** (−24 to +24 dB) scales only the key's trace, to compare a quiet key with a loud
  signal. It changes nothing you hear.
- **Layout** puts both traces on one axis (**Overlay**) or the key in the lower half (**Stacked**).
- **Source** and **Mid/Side** pick the channel for both traces.

A typical use: put Lens on the bass, key it from the kick, set Sync to 1 bar, and see whether the
bass ducks where the kick hits.

### Bigger window

The button at the right of the view tabs opens Lens in its own window, which you can resize or move
to another display. It shows the same views and controls as the card, and both follow each other.
The window closes when you delete the device or open another project.

## Waterfall

<Shot id="devices/audio-effects/lens/waterfall" alt="Nota Lens, Waterfall view" />

The spectrum over time, scrolling — see resonances ring, reverb tails decay and drum patterns repeat.
**Contrast** and brightness set how much detail shows. Next to the graph's title: how much time one
row covers.

## Common controls

**Freeze** (right of the view tabs) stops the display. **Source** picks what is analysed (on the
Display tab for Spectrum and Waterfall, on the Scope tab for the scope). **Top** and **Range** on
the Display tab set the vertical view; the two sliders on the Scale rail set them too, and on the
scope they set volts and time per division. The status line carries loudness and the measurements.
