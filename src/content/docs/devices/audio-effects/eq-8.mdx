Nota EQ-8 is a mixing-console equalizer with eight bands. Use it to clean up mud, cut rumble,
add air, carve space for one sound around another — on the stereo pair, the mid or the side.

<Shot id="devices/audio-effects/eq-8/card" alt="Nota EQ-8 with the band chips, the response graph and the band panel" />

## The graph

Each band is a numbered node on the response curve — drag it: sideways for frequency, up and
down for gain. The band chips above the graph switch bands on and off and select one for the
panel on the right.

## The analyzer

Behind the curve, the spectrum shows where the energy of the sound sits. **Analyzer** in the
corner picks what it shows:

- **Post** (the default): the sound after the EQ as a filled shape, with the sound going in as a
  dim line over it, so the EQ's effect reads as the gap between the two.
- **Pre**: the sound going in, filled.
- **Off**: nothing, and no analysis runs.

Each point reads a sixth of an octave, so a steady tone shows at its own level and pink noise
reads flat. The shape rises at once on a hit and falls back over about a third of a second.

- **60 / 90 / 120** sets the range: the graph's bottom edge is that many dB below 0 dBFS (90 by
  default). The EQ curve's own ±dB scale stays the same.
- **Peak** adds a thin line at each frequency's highest level of the last moments. It holds for
  1.5 seconds, then falls.

Range and Peak are saved with the device and in its presets. They change only the display,
never the sound.

## A band

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| Type | **Low cut**, **Low shelf**, **Bell**, **Notch**, **High shelf**, **High cut** |
| **Freq** · **Gain** · **Q** | Where, how much, how wide (on the cuts, **Reso**) |
| **Slope** | For the cuts: 12, 24 or 48 dB per octave |
| **Channel** | **St** (both channels), **Mid**, **Side**, **L** or **R** — so a band can brighten only the sides, or tame one channel |

## Band labels and notes

Give a band a name so its purpose shows at a glance: select it and type into **Label** at the top
of the band panel (up to 24 characters). The name appears next to the band's node in the graph,
follows the node as you drag it, and replaces the band type on its chip.

For the longer story, click the label in the graph, or **Note** next to the Label field. A box
opens with the label and a note of up to 2000 characters, line breaks included. Click outside,
press **Esc** or **⌘ Enter** (**Ctrl+Enter** on Windows and Linux) to close it; the text is kept.
A band with a note shows a small dot on its label, and hovering the label shows the note.

- Labels that would overlap are moved apart, with a thin line back to their node.
- **Labels** in the analyzer bar hides all labels and note dots in the graph (the chips and the
  band panel still show them). It is saved with the device.
- Labels and notes are saved with the project and in your own presets, and come along when you
  duplicate the device or the track. Each edit is one undo step. A factory preset or **Init**
  clears them, and double-clicking empty space for a new bell starts that band without a label.
- They never change the sound, and can't be automated.

## Global

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Scale** | Multiplies every shelf and bell gain at once, 0–200 % — 50 % halves the whole curve |
| **Output** | Output trim |
| **Auto gain** | Compensates the average level change of the shelves and bells, so you judge an edit at the same loudness |

## Presets

34 factory presets: tone (**Bass Boost**, **Air & Presence**, **Smile Curve**, **Loudness Contour**,
**Master Tilt**), cleanup (**Low Cut**, **Sub Rumble Filter**, **Mud Cut**, **Hum Removal 50 Hz** /
**60 Hz**, **Mono Low End**), mix (**Clean Mix Bus**, **Pad Tuck**, **Double-Track Split**, **Wide
Air**, **Focus Center**), vocals (**Vocal Presence**, **De-Box Vocal**, **Sibilance Tamer**,
**Podcast Voice**), drums (**Kick Punch**, **Snare Crack**, **Overheads Air**, **Hi-Hat Clean**),
instruments (**Bass Definition**, **Acoustic Guitar**, **Electric Guitar Cab**, **Piano Clarity**)
and colour (**Telephone**, **Lo-Fi Radio**, **Band Pass**).

## Tips

- **Cut narrow, boost wide:** find a harsh frequency with a narrow, boosted bell, then flip it
  to a cut.
- **Mono lows:** a low cut on the **Side** band around 120 Hz keeps the bass centred.
