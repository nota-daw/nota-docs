Nota EQ-8 is a mixing-console equalizer with eight bands. Use it to clean up mud, cut rumble,
add air, carve space for one sound around another — on the stereo pair, the mid or the side.

<Shot id="devices/audio-effects/eq-8/card" alt="Nota EQ-8: the row of band chips over the response graph on the left, with Analyzer and Auto gain in its corner, and a narrow column on the right with the picked band's settings, Scale and Output" />

## Layout

The graph leads: a slim row of chips for the eight bands sits on top of the response graph, with
**Analyzer** and **Auto gain** in its top-left corner. The narrow panel on the right edits the
picked band — its type, **Freq**, **Gain** and **Q**, **Slope** and **Channel** — and ends with the
device's **Scale** and **Output**.

The arrow at the top right of the panel folds it to a thin strip, so the card narrows and the
rest of the chain gets more room. Folded, the graph and the band chips keep working (right-click a
node for its type, slope and channel); click the arrow again to bring the panel back. The fold
changes no setting and isn't saved with the project.

## The graph and the band chips

Each band is a numbered node on the response curve — drag it: sideways for frequency, up and
down for gain. Its frequency shows on the node and in the tooltip.

Over the graph each band has a chip with its **number**, its filter **shape** and its frequency.
Click a chip to pick the band for the panel on the right (the outlined chip's settings show
there); double-click it to switch the band on or off — a band that's off is greyed out. A setting that
doesn't apply to the band's type — **Slope** on a shelf — is greyed out with nothing picked.

## A band

The panel opens with the band's number, its type and an **On** switch.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| Type | **Low cut**, **Low shelf**, **Bell**, **Notch**, **High shelf**, **High cut** — the six shapes under the band's name; hover one for its name |
| **Freq** · **Gain** · **Q** | Where, how much, how wide (on the cuts, **Reso**) |
| **Slope** | For the cuts: 12, 24 or 48 dB per octave |
| **Channel** | **St** (both channels), **M** (mid), **S** (side), **L** or **R** — so a band can brighten only the sides, or tame one channel |

## Global

**Analyzer** and **Auto gain** sit over the graph; **Scale** and **Output** close the panel.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Analyzer** | Shows the spectrum **Pre** or **Post** EQ behind the curve, or **Off** |
| **Scale** | Multiplies every shelf and bell gain at once, 0–200 % — 50 % halves the whole curve |
| **Output** | Output trim |
| **Auto gain** | Compensates the average level change of the shelves and bells, so you judge an edit at the same loudness |

## Presets

34 factory presets: tone (**Bass Boost**, **Air & Presence**, **Smile Curve**, **Loudness Contour**,
**Master Tilt**), cleanup (**Low Cut**, **Sub Rumble Filter**, **Mud Cut**, **Hum Removal 50 Hz** /
**60 Hz**, **Mono Low End**), mix (**Clean Mix Bus**, **Pad Tuck**, **Double-Track Split**, **Wide
Air**, **Focus Center**), vocals (**Vocal Presence**, **De-Box Vocal**, **Sibilance Tamer**,
**Podcast Voice**), drums (**Kick Punch**, **Snare Crack**, **Overheads Air**, **Hi-Hat Clean**),
instruments (**Bass Definition**, **Acoustic Guitar**, **Electric Guitar Cab**, **Piano Clarity**)
and colour (**Telephone**, **Lo-Fi Radio**, **Band Pass**).

## Tips

- **Cut narrow, boost wide:** find a harsh frequency with a narrow, boosted bell, then flip it
  to a cut.
- **Mono lows:** a low cut on a band set to **S** (side) around 120 Hz keeps the bass centred.
