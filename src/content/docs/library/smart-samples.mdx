The **Files** tab shows your Samples folder (set in [Settings ▸ Library](../../preferences/library/))
as a folder tree, and Nota knows what's in it.

<Shot id="library/files-tab" alt="The Files tab with filter chips and folders" width={300} />

## What Nota learns

In the background, Nota works out each file's **tempo**, **key**, and whether it's a **loop** or a
**one-shot**, plus a timbre fingerprint. A newly installed pack goes first; results are kept, so each
file is analysed once. The status line shows progress.

- Names come first: `Loop_124_Am`, `Pad C#m 90bpm` or a `One Shots` folder win over the analysis.
- A loop's exact tempo comes from its length — 16 beats in 7.742 s is 124.0.

Rows show it at the right edge: `124 · Am` for a loop, the note for a tuned hit.

## Filtering

The chips under the search box narrow the list:

- **Loops** · **One-shots**
- **BPM** — a range, with **project tempo ±5 %** one click away (loops that close warp cleanly)
- **Key** — with the project key one click away; a key also matches its relative, so A minor finds C
  major loops too

## Similar sounds

Right-click a sample ▸ **Show similar sounds** lists the closest samples of the same kind, closest
first. Click the chip to go back to all samples.

## Loops that fit

Drop a loop on the Arrangement and it's **warped to the project tempo** from the tempo Nota already
knows (⋮ ▸ **Warp loops to project tempo**, on by default) — the status line says so: "loop warped 124
→ 120 BPM". With ⋮ ▸ **Transpose to project key** on, it also moves to the
[project key](../../basics/transport/) the shorter way. One-shots are never warped.

MIDI files (`.mid`) show in the Files tab too, and drop like samples — see
[Projects and saving](../../basics/projects/).
